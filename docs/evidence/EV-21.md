# EV-21: does lexical clustering find topics in this corpus, or does it find the template again?

> **PRE-REGISTRATION.** Everything above the `## Measurement` rule was written and committed
> BEFORE any clustering was run, as `asc-h7d` requires ("Name the question BEFORE running").
> The predictions are numbered so that each can be marked hit or missed rather than reinterpreted.
> Commit history is the receipt.

> **Project identifiers are redacted.** This repository is public. Private project names are
> replaced with stable pseudonyms; the same pseudonym always means the same project.

**Question**    `asc-h7d` wants "read 500 entries" to become "read 12 clusters, three examples
                each", and asks for two numbers: **the judged coherence rate of the top 10
                clusters, and the losing configuration's numbers.**

                EV-20 makes the question sharper than the bead could have known. It measured that
                the strongest lexical signal in this corpus's prose is not a topic at all — it is
                43 words of AskUserQuestion boilerplate carried in 10 of 97 entries, which produced
                **100% of every near-duplicate merge in that record.** TF-IDF does not delete that
                signal; a term in 10 of 97 documents has `log(97/10) = 2.27` of weight, not zero.

                So the question is not only "does clustering find topics" but **"does it find
                topics, or does it rediscover the template EV-20 found?"** — and this record
                predicts, in advance, that it finds the template.

## Method

**The corpus.** The same one EV-20 used, unchanged: `asc export` over the real store, the 97
entries carrying any prose (`evidence_text`, `decision.rationale`, `note.text`, `decision.chosen`),
which is 5.4% of 1,795. Same tokenizer: lowercase, split on any non-alphanumeric run. No stopword
list — `cluster.ts` uses `idf = log(N/df)`, which sends a term in every document to exactly zero.

**Arms.** `cluster` from `packages/analysis/src/cluster.ts`, sublinear tf, identical in every
respect but the linkage:

- **Arm A — average linkage (the candidate to ship).**
- **Arm B — single linkage (expected to lose by chaining).**
- **Arm C — complete linkage (expected to lose by fragmenting).**

**Choosing the cut.** `sweepThreshold` over 0.50 … 0.98, one dendrogram per linkage, cut
repeatedly. The cut is chosen by the rule fixed here: **the threshold maximising mean silhouette
among cuts that leave at least three non-singleton clusters.** The constraint is there because a
cut leaving one giant cluster and a cut leaving 97 singletons both score 0, and neither is an
answer to the bead's question.

**Disbelieving the result.** `clusterPermutationNull`, 199 shuffles, seeded. The shuffle pools
every token in the corpus, permutes it, and deals it back so each document keeps its own length —
vocabulary, per-term frequency and document lengths all survive exactly, and only co-occurrence is
destroyed.

**Adjudication, defined before reading anything.** For each of the top 10 clusters by size (all of
them, if there are fewer than 10):

> A cluster is **COHERENT** if one sentence can be written that describes what every member is
> about, and that sentence is more specific than the entry type's own name. "These are all
> `user_correction` entries" does not count. "These are all about how to fund a test wallet" does.

**Coherence rate = coherent clusters / clusters judged.** Singletons are not judged and not
counted, in either direction.

## Predictions

1. **The observed mean silhouette beats every one of the 199 shuffles** (p = 0.005, the smallest
   available), for average linkage at the chosen cut. 97 hand-written entries about genuinely
   different subjects should be separable when a shuffled version of themselves is not.
2. **Fewer than 10 non-singleton clusters exist at the chosen cut.** N=97 with most entries
   one-off; the top-10 list will not fill.
3. **Average linkage's judged coherence rate is at least 0.6.**
4. **One cluster will be the AskUserQuestion preamble cluster** — three or more `user_correction`
   entries whose only commonality is the 43-word template — **and it will be judged INCOHERENT.**
   This is the prediction this record exists to make. If it is a hit, TF-IDF clustering rediscovers
   `asc-m4u` without being told about it.
5. **Single linkage is the losing arm and loses by chaining:** its largest cluster at the chosen
   cut is at least twice the size of average linkage's, and its coherence rate is lower.
6. **Complete linkage loses by fragmenting:** more clusters and more singletons than average
   linkage at the same cut.

**Decision rule, fixed in advance.** Ship `average` as the default linkage if its coherence rate is
the highest of the three arms; ship the threshold the sweep rule selects. If the observed
silhouette does not beat the permutation null, ship no default threshold and say the technique does
not separate this corpus — a negative result, and a successful task.

## Measurement

Run 2026-09-18 against the real store. 97 prose entries, `cluster` from
`packages/analysis/src/cluster.ts`, sublinear tf, `sweepThreshold` over 0.50 … 0.98 in steps of
0.02.

### The sweep, and the cut each arm selected

Selection rule as fixed above: highest mean silhouette among cuts leaving at least three
non-singleton clusters.

| linkage | cut selected | clusters | singletons | non-singleton | largest | silhouette |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **average** | **0.90** | 35 | 10 | 25 | 10 | **0.084922** |
| single | 0.80 | 63 | 48 | 15 | 10 | 0.065088 |
| complete | 0.88 | 56 | 29 | 27 | 10 | **0.091610** |

At a **common** cut of 0.90, where the three are directly comparable:

| linkage | clusters | singletons | largest | silhouette |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| average | 35 | 10 | 10 | 0.084922 |
| single | 6 | 3 | **73** | 0.014590 |
| complete | 47 | 16 | 10 | 0.089102 |

### The permutation null

Average linkage at 0.90, 199 shuffles, seeded. The shuffle keeps the vocabulary, every term's
frequency and every document's length; only co-occurrence is destroyed.

```
observed silhouette   0.084922
null min              0.002610
null median           0.006329
null max              0.013617
p                     0.005
```

The observed value is **6.2× the null's maximum** over 199 shuffles, and p is the smallest a
199-shuffle test can report.

### The losing arms, judged the same way

| arm | cut | non-singleton clusters | entries in a cluster | coherence rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **average** | 0.90 | 25 | **87 of 97 (89.7%)** | **9/10 = 0.90** |
| single | 0.80 | 15 | 49 of 97 (50.5%) | 8/10 = 0.80 |
| complete | 0.88 | 27 | 68 of 97 (70.1%) | 9/10 = 0.90 |

Single linkage's one extra failure is its cluster 2 (size 6, **silhouette −0.0104**), which merges
a full-gate-run entry, two module-landing entries, a mutation-testing receipt and an
`ARCHITECTURE.md` scheme-vocabulary decision. Average linkage splits exactly that material into its
clusters 3 and 4, both coherent. The negative silhouette flags it without being asked to.

## Predictions: hit or missed

1. **Observed mean silhouette beats every one of 199 shuffles** — **HIT.** 0.084922 against a null
   maximum of 0.013617; p = 0.005, the smallest available.
2. **Fewer than 10 non-singleton clusters at the chosen cut** — **MISSED**, and not narrowly: 25.
   The corpus is more topically structured than predicted.
3. **Average linkage's coherence rate is at least 0.6** — **HIT**, 0.90.
4. **One cluster is the AskUserQuestion preamble cluster, and it is INCOHERENT** — **HIT**, and
   more sharply than predicted. It is the **largest cluster in all three arms**, it holds exactly
   the 10 entries `asc-m4u` identified, and its top terms are the boilerplate itself: *questions,
   clarify, provided, they, account, answer, additional, appropriate*. TF-IDF clustering
   rediscovered `asc-m4u` from the text alone, with nothing told to it.
5. **Single linkage loses by chaining: largest cluster at least 2× average's, and a lower coherence
   rate** — **SPLIT, and the split is the interesting part.** Coherence: **HIT** (0.80 against
   0.90). Largest cluster: **MISSED at each arm's own cut** (10 against 10) and **HIT at a common
   cut of 0.90** (73 against 10, 7.3×). The reason is worth keeping: chaining destroys the
   silhouette, so the sweep rule refuses to select a cut where single linkage chains. The
   pre-registered selection rule protected the arm it was expected to expose.
6. **Complete linkage fragments: more clusters and more singletons than average at the same cut** —
   **HIT.** At 0.90: 47 clusters and 16 singletons against 35 and 10.

Four hits, one miss, one split.

## Decision

**Ship `average` as the default linkage, and `sweepThreshold` rather than a default cut.**

**The pre-registered rule under-determined the outcome for the second record running, and the
tiebreak is labelled as a post-hoc judgement.** The rule said ship average if its coherence rate is
the highest of the three; average and complete **tied at 0.90**. The tiebreak used is the bead's own
purpose — "read 500 entries" becomes "read 12 clusters" only for entries that are actually in a
cluster, and a singleton is an entry the reader still has to read alone:

- average places **87 of 97 entries (89.7%)** into a non-singleton cluster
- complete places **68 of 97 (70.1%)**
- single places **49 of 97 (50.5%)**

Complete linkage wins on separation (silhouette 0.091610 against 0.084922) and loses on coverage by
19 entries. For this bead, coverage is the thing being bought.

**No default threshold ships.** The selected cut moved between arms (0.90, 0.88, 0.80) and the
silhouette curve is flat across 0.82–0.92 for average linkage — 0.081972, 0.084086, 0.079134,
0.078669, 0.084922. Picking one of those as a constant would be picking a number off a flat curve
and calling it a finding. `sweepThreshold` exists so a caller chooses deliberately, and this is the
measurement that says why.

**A positive result, plainly: lexical clustering works on this corpus.** Unlike `asc-yce`, whose
instrument found nothing to do here, this one separates the corpus at 6.2× the permutation null and
nine of its ten largest clusters survive hand-reading. The bead's premise holds.

**And it rediscovered a defect it was not told about.** The largest cluster in every arm is
`asc-m4u`. EV-20 found that boilerplate by hand-reading near-duplicate merges; this record's
clustering surfaced the same 10 entries as its top result, ranked first by size. Two instruments,
independently, pointing at the same 43 words.

## Confidence

**High on the separation result.** The permutation null is 199 seeded shuffles preserving
vocabulary, term frequency and document length, and the observed value clears its maximum by 6.2×.
That is not a marginal p.

**Moderate on the coherence rates, and the reason is the denominator.** Ten clusters judged per
arm, by one reader, against a rule fixed in advance. 9/10 and 8/10 differ by one cluster; the gap
between average and single linkage is real in direction but its size rests on a single judgement
call. **Ten is under `MIN_N`** (20, `packages/analysis/src/proportion.ts:50`) and these rates are
reported as counts, not as estimates of a coherence rate for this or any corpus.

**One limitation this record found the hard way and should be read as a warning.** The silhouette
does not detect meaninglessness. The boilerplate cluster is the most lexically tight cluster in the
corpus — silhouette 0.2445, the highest of the ten judged — and it is the one cluster that is
worthless. A caller who ranks clusters by silhouette and reads the top one will read the template.
The score measures separation, which is exactly what it claims and less than a reader might hope.

**The coverage limit is the same one EV-20 hit.** 1,698 of 1,795 entries (94.6%) carry no prose,
so every number here describes 5.4% of the store.
