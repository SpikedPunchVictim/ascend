# EV-64: do the seven draft betterment types cover what people actually changed in their process?

**Status** Predictions registered 2026-10-13 in the notes of `asc-09wx.15`, before either rater
ran. Measured the same day.

**Question** `asc-09wx.15` drafted seven betterment types from this session's own examples (wasted
standing input, wasted waiting, undelivered guidance, repeated friction, escape with no guard,
unstable process, missing signal). Do those seven cover the process changes that four of our own
repositories and one public repository actually made, and can two raters apply them the same way?

## Method

- **Items:** 464 commits that touch a process file (hook, lint, test or CI configuration, agent
  instruction or skill file, mutation spec): every such commit in `ascend` (48), `grizzly-pl` (115),
  `mast` (18) and `spindle` (6), and the 277 commits since 2022-01-01 that CREATE such a file in
  `n8n`. Each item is a repository, a date, the process files touched and the commit subject. No
  diff was shown.
- **Labels:** ten. The seven types, `other_betterment` (with a two-to-five word name),
  `setup` (first-time scaffolding with no stated problem) and `incidental` (the process file moved
  as a side effect of product work).
- **Raters:** two model subagents given the same rubric and items, on different models, neither
  shown the other's output or the predictions.
- **Statistic:** Cohen's kappa over the ten labels; then separately over "a betterment or not" and
  over the type among items both raters call a betterment.

## Predictions

- **P1.** `setup` plus `incidental` is at least 50% of items.
- **P2.** In our own repositories the commonest betterment type is `escape_no_guard`.
- **P3.** In n8n it is also `escape_no_guard`, and `wasted_standing_input` is under 2% of n8n's
  betterments.
- **P4.** `other_betterment` is at least 10% of betterments.
- **P5.** The raters agree at kappa 0.6 or above.

## Decision

**The seven types hold up as names; the boundary of "a betterment" does not.**

1. **Naming the type is reliable once two raters agree something is a betterment**: kappa 0.896 on
   98 items. The types are distinguishable from a commit subject alone.
2. **Deciding whether a process change is a betterment at all is where raters part**: kappa 0.587,
   and the largest single disagreement (83 items) is `setup` against `incidental`, two labels that
   both mean "not a betterment" and should be one.
3. **One type is missing: a manual chore automated.** Sixteen of rater A's 24 `other_betterment`
   items are release, hygiene, routing, preview or other manual-step automation, and rater B filed nearly
   all of them under `setup`. That is an eighth type, "repeated manual step", and it is the reason
   P4 split.
4. **`escape_no_guard` dominates what people did** (48 of the 91 agreed betterment labels), and
   `wasted_standing_input` is nearly absent (2, both ours). The types this session found by
   measuring cost are not the ones history shows people fixing, which is the argument for
   detecting them: nobody was looking.

Changes to the draft: merge `setup` and `incidental` into one "not a betterment" label, add
"repeated manual step" as an eighth type, and require a stated problem (in the subject or the
diff) before an item counts as a betterment.

## Confidence

**Medium** that the type names are separable, **low** for any count per type. Both raters are
models, not people; they saw subjects and file names, not diffs; n8n contributes file creations
only, which favours `setup`; and the five repositories are one owner's four and one public
project. `repeated_friction` (1 agreed) and `wasted_standing_input` (2 agreed) are anecdotes.
