# EV-46: does the corpus tests' load-induced red have a remedy inside this repo?

**Question.** `asc-pcaw` is the third occurrence of one class — `dogfood/0007`, then `asc-3x1`, then
`dogfood/0063`, where a full-suite run at `loadavg 95.34` timed out `normalize-real-corpus.test.ts`
at `duration_ms 180086` against a bound of 180000, on a sweep that takes 29489 ms on a quiet machine.
The red blocks a commit unrelated to it, and `dogfood/0063` names the cost plainly: *"`--no-verify` is
one keystroke away from this red."*

The bead pre-registered three remedies and one alternative, and this record tests them:

1. a bounded retry for the corpus tests,
2. a load-aware guard,
3. the corpus files moved into their own **serialized project** —
4. or an explicit decision to leave it, recorded with the measured margin.

**The premise of remedy 3 was already on record and was the reason to try it first.** `dogfood/0007`
measured the corpus files at **114.01 s racing and 75.00 s run sequentially**, and `dogfood/0063`
quoted that as *"evidence for the third"*. If the corpus files are slowed by running concurrently,
a serialized project removes the slowdown. So the premise is testable before anything is built, and
testing it costs one `vitest run` rather than a restructure of the config the pre-commit gate depends
on.

**Method.** 2026-10-09, this checkout, macOS, 12 cores. Three arrangements of the same three corpus
files, then a throttling ladder against one of them. Timings from `/usr/bin/time -p` around
`./node_modules/.bin/vitest run`; per-file durations from the JSON reporter's `startTime`/`endTime`.
`loadavg` from `sysctl -n vm.loadavg`. Nothing was built or changed for this measurement.

## Measurement 1 — the premise, and it does not hold

| arrangement | derive | normalize | reader | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the trio alone, default parallelism | 31.6 s | 23.9 s | 28.2 s | **32.31 s** |
| the trio alone, `--no-file-parallelism` | 31.5 s | 23.6 s | 27.5 s | **84.17 s** |
| the full suite (137 files, 3179 tests) | — | **23.588 s** | — | **189.61 s** |

**Every file's own duration is the same in all three.** Normalize's top test took **23560 ms alone,
23560 ms with its two siblings racing, and 23560 ms inside the full suite** — to the millisecond at
that resolution. The corpus files do not contend with each other and they do not contend with the
other 129 files. The wall-time difference between the first two rows is nothing but concurrency:
three files on six free forks finish in 32 s, the same three run one after another take 84 s.

So serializing them **costs 52 s of gate time and removes nothing**. `dogfood/0007`'s 114 → 75 s
measurement does not reproduce on today's tree; whatever produced it — the forks regime before
`asc-3x1`, or a loaded machine at the time — is not the condition this tree is in now. Remedy 3 is
refuted by its own premise.

The third row also settles the wider question. The corpus test is **not** slowed by the suite it runs
in, which means the 6.1× spread recorded on 2026-10-05 cannot have come from inside the run at all.

## Measurement 2 — what it actually takes to make it red

One file, `normalize-real-corpus.test.ts`, alone, against its 180000 ms bound. Idle reference is the
23.9 s above.

| what was done to the test | wall | vs idle | `loadavg` at start | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nothing | 23.9 s | 1.0× | 2.77 | green |
| `taskpolicy -b` (background QoS band) | 67.13 s | 2.8× | 7.09 | green |
| `-b` plus 8 × `nice -n 20` spinners | 63.55 s | 2.7× | 47.38 | green |
| `-b -c maintenance` (lowest QoS clamp) | 69.40 s | 2.9× | 19.38 | green |
| `-b` plus 16 × `nice -n 20` spinners | 322.51 s | 13.5× | 7.55 | **red**, but not this red |

The four throttling mechanisms **plateau at ~2.8×**, and the last row's red is a different failure:
`[vitest-worker]: Timeout calling "fetch" with "[\"/packages/core/src/spec.ts\",\"ssr\"]"` — the worker
never got enough CPU to transform a module, and the run failed having executed **zero** tests. It is
a load-caused red that blocks a run, which is the class, but it is not the corpus test's own bound
firing and it is not offered as a reproduction of `dogfood/0063`.

**The mechanism, stated so it can be falsified.** Starvation here is `runnable threads / cores`. The
recorded red is 180086 / 29489 = **6.1×** at `loadavg 95.34` on **12** cores, i.e. **7.9×**
oversubscription — the two numbers agree, which is the whole of it: the sweep got about one seventh
of a core. Nothing smaller reproduces it.

**And the load has to be at the test's own priority to count.** That is why the ladder plateaus.
`loadavg` counts runnable threads regardless of scheduling class, so the 8-spinner row reached
`loadavg 47.38` — 3.9× oversubscription — and the test still ran at 2.6×, because a `nice -n 20`
thread never outranks a normal-priority one and is simply not scheduled while the test wants CPU.
A QoS band has the same shape: it throttles only under contention, and on an idle machine a clamped
process still gets a core. **Reaching 6.1× needs ≈73 runnable threads at the test's own priority on
12 cores** — that is, the machine genuinely oversubscribed by other work, which is what the original
`loadavg 95.34` was. It is not reachable by lowering the test's priority, and the user declined the
~90-spinner run that would reach it.

This also sharpens the instrument `asc-9ac` built. `vitest.failure-log.ts` records `loadavg` with each
failure, and the memory derived from it says to read that number before believing a diagnosis. The
number is real but it is **not a measure of the competition the test faced** — it cannot tell a busy
machine from a quiet one with low-priority work on it. Two of this ladder's rows sit at `loadavg` 47
and 19 and behave identically.

## Remedy 2 is closed by the machinery that already exists

A load-aware guard would skip or defer the corpus tests when the machine is busy, which is the only
one of the three that could actually make the red go away, since the cause is external to the repo.
It is closed by `scripts/test-baseline.mjs`, which `asc-049w` built for exactly this: `scan.passed` is
documented there as *"what a `it.skip` does not move"*, and its check reports a fall as
*"a test skipped or gone, not necessarily deleted"*. Skipping the corpus tests under load does not
remove the red; it converts it into the baseline's red.

**With one honest caveat.** The baseline is a floor and it lags: it was last written 2026-10-05 and
reads 132 files / 3049 tests / 3047 passed, against today's 137 / 3179 / 3177. That 130-test cushion
is wide enough that skipping ~20 corpus tests today would pass the floor unnoticed; it would be
caught after the next `pnpm test:baseline`. The drift is the documented, accepted cost of floors, not
a defect — but it means the guard is closed by a check that is currently slack, and worth saying so
rather than claiming a trap that would not spring this week.

## Decision

**No remedy was taken; the decision to leave it is recorded, per the bead's own alternative.** The
measured margin is **180086 ms red against 29489 ms green on the same tree** — the number the bead
names — and the reason is not that no remedy was tried: it is that the cause is outside the repo, and
each of the three pre-registered remedies either removes something that is not there or replaces the
red with a different one.

| remedy | what it would have to remove | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| corpus files in a serialized project | contention between the corpus files and the rest of the run | **no such contention exists** (Measurement 1); it would cost 32.31 s → 84.17 s and remove nothing |
| a bounded retry | the load, between attempt 1 and attempt 2 | a retry re-runs at the same load; and the recorded reds do not track load — `dogfood/0063` recorded that the slowest of its four was on the *least*-loaded machine |
| a load-aware guard | the red itself | the count baseline exists to catch a skipped test and would, once refreshed; it substitutes the baseline's red for the test's |
| record the decision | nothing | **taken** |

What would actually change the outcome is external to the repository: the gate is blocked when the
machine is 6× oversubscribed, and the only in-repo levers are the three above. Raising the 180 s
bound was considered and rejected twice already (`dogfood/0007`, `asc-9ac`) on the ground that the
sweep does ~10 s of real work and a larger bound hides the regression the tripwire exists to catch.
That reasoning is unchanged, and this record adds no reason to revisit it.

**What this does not do.** It does not stop the gate reddening under load, and it does not make the
red cheaper when it happens. If that cost is judged too high, the remaining lever is a design one —
whether a corpus check that is honest only on an unloaded machine belongs in a commit-blocking gate
at all — and that is a question about the gate's composition, not about the corpus tests' bound.
It is named here rather than acted on.

**Confidence.** `Confirmed (empirical)` for both tables: every row is a real `vitest run` on this
tree, timed externally and corroborated by the runner's own per-file durations. `Traced` for the
`test-baseline.mjs` interaction — the check and its message were read, not executed against a
skipped run. **Not established, and stated as unestablished:** the exact `Test timed out in 180000ms`
red was not reproduced. Four throttling mechanisms were tried and all plateaued at ~2.8×; the
reproduction needs ~6× oversubscription at the test's own priority, which the gentle arm the user
chose cannot reach. The ladder's own numbers were taken with the machine between `loadavg` 7 and 47,
not at the 2.77 of the idle reference, so the plateau is if anything understated.

## Addendum, 2026-10-13 — the design question is answered

`asc-pi5a` carried this record's closing question forward as its own task. On 2026-10-13 the owner
answered it, and the answer is to **keep the corpus checks on the commit-blocking path** — option (a),
not the "move them off the commit path" shape this record named and did not act on. Nothing above
changes: the three in-repo remedies stay refuted by Measurement 1 and Measurement 2, the 180 s bound
stays rejected, and the gate may still redden for a reason external to the diff. The decision is that
that cost is accepted and `--no-verify` remains the deliberate, visible escape rather than an
unstated default. Recorded in the bead `asc-pi5a`; no source, hook, or bound changed.
