# EV-52: can the corpus attribute a fall in permission-rule denials to an allowlist change?

**Status**      Pre-registered 2026-10-13 for `asc-3936`. Everything from this line to the end of
                "Predictions" was written, and sealed by sha256 in the bead's notes, before any
                pre/post count was run. The result sections below the seal are added afterwards.

**Question**    EV-3 and EV-19 found that permission-rule denials concentrate, and named the action:
                allowlist entries. Allowlists then grew. Can the corpus say whether denials fell
                *because* of that? This is the README's motivating sentence turned on the tool's own
                first finding: "when a workflow does change its process, there's no way to tell
                whether the change helped."

## What was already known when the predictions were written

Stated because a pre-registration is only as clean as what its author had seen.

- **The outcome has been partly seen.** A reviewing agent reported, earlier in the same session,
  permission-rule denials per session of 8.77 (Aug), 6.36 (Sep), 3.54 (Oct) over ALL projects. Those
  three numbers were not re-verified and mix projects, but the direction was known. No per-project,
  per-day or pre/post count had been run.
- **Per-project totals have been seen**, not their split over time: `tool_denial` holds 214 entries
  for this project over 23 days and 5 sessions, 155 for `grizzly-exp` over 31 days and 9 sessions,
  138 for `grizzly-wip` over 30 days and 6 sessions (all denial kinds, invalidated excluded).
- **The intervention is undated.** The allowlists live in untracked `.claude/settings.local.json`
  files, so the only date available is the file's last-modified time, which dates the LAST addition
  and says nothing about the others:

  | project       | allow entries | file born  | last modified    |
  | ------------- | ------------- | ---------- | ---------------- |
  | `ascend`      | 36            | 2026-08-17 | 2026-09-21 10:13 |
  | `grizzly-wip` | 26            | 2026-08-12 | 2026-09-01       |
  | `grizzly-exp` | 0             | 2026-09-25 | 2026-09-27       |

  The last entry in ascend's file is `Bash(EV16_MODEL=*)`, and EV-16's arms ran on 2026-09-21, so
  that edit may be one harness rule and not a response to EV-3 at all. No `decision` entry, bead or
  evidence record says an allowlist was changed because of a finding.

## Method (fixed here)

- **Outcome:** `tool_denial` entries whose `denial_kind` is the permission-rule value, with
  `invalidated` unset, read through `asc query` from `v_tool_denial_v1`, clock `occurred_at`.
- **Unit:** one *active day* of one project: a UTC date on which that project has at least one
  derived entry of any type. The session is not used: the three projects have 5, 6 and 9 sessions
  with a denial, all under `MIN_N` (20, `packages/analysis/src/proportion.ts`). A day with activity
  and no permission-rule denial counts as a unit with value 0.
- **Arms:** two treated series and one control.
  - `ascend`, split at 2026-09-21: pre is every active day before it, post every active day after.
  - `grizzly-wip`, split at 2026-09-01, likewise.
  - `grizzly-exp`, control (no allow entries in either settings file), split at BOTH dates.
- **Washout:** the split date itself is excluded from both sides, since the edit happened at some
  hour inside it. 2026-10-13 is excluded as a partial day.
- **Statistic:** mean permission-rule denials per active day, post minus pre. Tested by a
  two-sided permutation test over days, 10,000 shuffles of the pre/post labels, seed 20261013.
- **The shipped instrument is run too:** `asc stats tool_denial --changepoints --at occurred_at
  --by denial_kind`, under both `--method pettitt` and `--method cusum`, as it stands.

**The rule for "the corpus attributes the fall to the allowlist"**, all three required for a
treated series:

1. both its pre and post sides hold at least `MIN_N` (20) active days;
2. its post-minus-pre difference is negative with permutation p < 0.05;
3. the control, split at the same date, does not show a negative difference with p < 0.05.

Anything else is reported as "cannot attribute", and a side under `MIN_N` is named an anecdote
whatever its p-value.

## Predictions

- **P1 (the unit).** At least one of the four treated sides (ascend pre, ascend post, grizzly-wip
  pre, grizzly-wip post) holds fewer than 20 active days, so condition 1 fails for both treated
  series and the answer is "cannot attribute" before any p-value is read. Specifically: ascend's
  pre side holds at most 10 active days and grizzly-wip's at most 13.
  *Falsifier:* either treated series has 20 or more active days on both sides.
- **P2 (direction).** In both treated series the post mean is lower than the pre mean.
  *Falsifier:* either treated series has a post mean equal to or above its pre mean.
- **P3 (the control).** The control does NOT separate the treated series from drift: at one or
  both split dates `grizzly-exp`'s post mean is also lower than its pre mean.
  *Falsifier:* `grizzly-exp`'s post mean is at or above its pre mean at both split dates.
- **P4 (the shipped instrument).** `asc stats --changepoints` cannot ask the question as posed: it
  has no way to restrict a series to one project, and the changepoint it reports for the
  permission-rule series, under either method, is not within 3 days of 2026-09-21.
  *Falsifier:* the command accepts a project restriction, or either method places the
  permission-rule changepoint in 2026-09-18..2026-09-24.

## Decision

**Cannot attribute, and there is no fall to attribute.** The rule's first condition fails for both
treated series, so the answer was fixed before a p-value was read. The more useful result is the one
P2 got wrong: within a project, permission-rule denials per active day did not go down after either
allowlist's last edit.

Three things follow.

1. **The monthly decline is a change in which sessions are counted, not in how often a session is
   denied.** Denials went 114, 176, 92 while sessions went 11, 21, 20 across a shifting set of
   projects. A per-session ratio over all projects fell because the denominator gained sessions from
   projects with few denials, and no per-project series shows it.
2. **The finding never applied to this project.** ascend has 8 permission-rule denials in 31 active
   days. EV-3's action ("allowlist entries") was derived from a corpus dominated by other projects,
   and the one allowlist with a known last-edit date inside that corpus is the one with nothing to
   fix.
3. **What blocked attribution is that the intervention was never recorded.** Two settings files
   changed and nothing in the store, the tracker or the evidence series says when, what, or why. A
   changepoint scan was meant to answer "did something improve?" from the data alone
   (`ARCHITECTURE.md`, "Statistical layer"); at this volume it cannot, and without a dated
   intervention there is no second route.

To the instrument's credit: the shipped changepoint scan reported no break in the permission-rule
series under either method, which is the right answer. It did not manufacture the effect that a
monthly ratio suggested.

## Confidence

**High** that neither treated series fell and that the unit is under `MIN_N`: the counts are whole
populations read from the live store, and the day series is small enough to check by eye.

**Low** for anything about what an allowlist does. The split dates are last-modified times of
untracked files, the pre sides are 9 and 12 days, and one user on one machine produced all of it.
This record does not show that allowlists do not help; it shows this corpus cannot say.

**Limitations.** The active-day unit ignores how much work a day held, so a quiet day and a heavy
one weigh the same. `occurred_at` is UTC and the file times are local, which the one-day washout
covers only if the edit and its UTC date agree. The outcome's direction over all projects was known
before the predictions were written, as stated above.
