# EV-15: what should a search that finds nothing say?

**Question**    `asc search <type> "<text>"` is BM25-ranked search over `evidence_text`, and the spec
                asks for a *"mast-style zero-result assist (nearest actual property values when a
                filter matches nothing)"*. Three questions, and the second and third are about claims
                the finished command makes to its caller:

                1. **What is a zero result actually a fact about?** The assist exists to stop a dead
                   end. Building one requires knowing which dead ends occur, and the spec's phrasing
                   assumes one of them.
                2. **Can a result that was limited claim to be complete?** `Output.coverage` defaults
                   to `complete(rows)`, which asserts the rows shown are the whole result.
                3. **What does porting the mast mechanism actually buy, on this corpus?** The
                   mechanism it names is trigram similarity over an index's vocabulary.

**Method**      The `docs/evidence/EV-11` frozen corpus (`/tmp/sx7/corpus/.ascend/ascend.db`, 1,491
                entries over nine types), driven through the real `asc` binary and, for the cost
                figures, through the store's own compiled functions. Read-only throughout: the corpus
                was opened, never written to.

                **Q1** measured the two counts that can make a search impossible -- entries per type,
                and how many of them the FTS index holds -- for every populated type, then drove the
                binary at the case the numbers predict.

                **Q2** drove `--limit 1` on a query with a known match count and read the coverage
                block back, comparing it against a `COUNT(*)` over the same match expression.

                **Q3** built the vocabulary of the indexed documents in JS and scored real
                transposition and deletion typos against it with mast's own metric -- a Dice coefficient
                over character trigram **sets**, lowercased, unpadded -- at the floor of 0.3 its source
                uses.

                The failure cases the corpus cannot produce -- an empty type, an unindexed type -- were
                driven against a purpose-built three-type scratch fixture, because a corpus with 486
                entries of one type cannot be made to hold no entries.

**Measurement**

**Q1 -- a zero result is a fact about the type far more often than about the query, and the spec's
assist would have been wrong about which.** The per-type counts, measured:

| type | entries | in the index |
|---|---|---|
| `verification_run` | 486 | **0** |
| `tool_denial` | 457 | **0** |
| `context_compaction` | 441 | **0** |
| `skill_activation` | 87 | **0** |
| `user_correction` | 20 | **20** |

**1,471 of 1,491 entries are unsearchable, and they are not spread evenly -- they are four of the five
populated types, in full.** For every one of those four, `asc search <type> <anything>` returns zero:
the query is irrelevant, because there is no document for it to match. This is not a corpus quirk so
much as the ordinary consequence of indexing one field: `evidence_text` is set when a workflow records
evidence, and a `tool_denial` or a `context_compaction` is recorded from an event that has none.

That makes the *"did you mean"* assist the wrong first answer here. A caller told "try a different
word" will try different words forever. The measured shape instead:

```
$ asc search verification_run "cargo"
id  score  snippet
--  -----  -------

no matches.
This type has 486 entries, and the index holds none of them: the index covers
'evidence_text', and no entry of this type carries any. No query can match, so retrying
with different words will not help -- the type's properties are where its content is.

The query terms do occur as property values, which a search does not cover:
  runner = "cargo test"  (127 entries)
  runner = "cargo clippy"  (88 entries)
  runner = "cargo check"  (21 entries)
```

The type is an unconditional zero and the term occurs **215 times in a property**. Both halves are
measurements; together they turn a dead end into a redirect, which is the whole point of the feature.

**Q3 -- the vocabulary pass is the part of the mast mechanism that does not survive the port.** The
indexed documents hold **592 distinct terms, 442 of them in exactly one document** (median document
frequency 1). Scoring real typos against that vocabulary with mast's own metric and floor:

| typo | candidates at ≥ 0.3 | best |
|---|---|---|
| `contxt` | 3 | `context` (0.44), `control` (0.44), `contract` (0.40) |
| `permision` | 3 | `permissions` (0.75), `decision` (0.46), `provisioning` (0.35) |
| `transcipt` | **0** | — |

A deletion typo finds its target, ambiguously; a **transposition** finds nothing, because swapping
two adjacent characters breaks three of eight trigrams and drops the score below the floor. The
floor itself is a bare `0.3` in that source with no recorded provenance, which is the kind of number
this project does not import. The pass is therefore not ported. What replaces it keeps the same idea
-- suggest only what exists -- and points it at the space ascend actually has, since a search here
covers one field of a typed entry rather than everything the entry holds.

**Q2 -- a limited result was claiming to be the whole answer, and the default made it invisible.**
Measured on the corpus: `the` against `user_correction` matches **17** entries. At `--limit 1`:

```
$ asc search user_correction "the" --limit 1 --json
…"row_count":1,"coverage":{"shown":1,"total":17,"has_more":true,"percent":5.9}}
```

The `total` is **counted**, not inferred, and that distinction is the fix. `Output.coverage` defaults
to `complete(rows)`, which asserts the rows shown are the whole result -- and under a `LIMIT` that is
false. With the default the same command reports `{"shown":1,"total":1,"has_more":false}`: a search
that found seventeen things claiming it found exactly one, in the single field a consumer reads to
decide whether it holds the whole answer. Nothing in the row list distinguishes the two cases, which
is why the denominator had to come from a second query. That query costs **0.0221 ms** (n = 200), and
its plan is an FTS index scan joined to `entries` by primary key, so it counts matched documents
rather than reading them.

**The costs, all measured on this corpus at n = 200:** the per-type indexed count is **0.0213 ms**;
the match count is **0.0221 ms**; the `json_each` property-value scan is **0.7275 ms** -- 33x the
others and still under a millisecond, which is why the assist can afford it on the zero-result path
and why nothing else pays for it. The plan for that scan is `SEARCH e USING INDEX
idx_prop_skill (type_name=?)` followed by `SCAN je VIRTUAL TABLE`, so the type filter is served by an
index and only that type's properties are walked.

**A hazard was found in the property match and reproduced before it was fixed.** Property values are
matched by containment, so the term goes into a `LIKE` pattern -- and the terms are runs of
`[\p{L}\p{N}_]+`, which means `_` can occur in a legitimate query. Unescaped, `LIKE '%___%'` matches
every value of three or more characters: measured on the corpus, a query of `___` returns **2,564
rows** -- every non-empty property value in the store -- as *suggestions*. The fix is a pair: the
pattern escapes `\`, `%` and `_` in JS and the SQL carries `ESCAPE '\'`. `%` turns out to be
unreachable by a stronger route and that was checked rather than assumed -- it is not a term
character, so `%zz%` tokenizes to `zz`, falls below the three-code-point floor, and is refused before
any SQL runs.

**Every assertion carrying the feature was mutation-tested**, per this project's rule that a check
must be shown to fail before it is trusted to pass. Twelve mutations across two rounds, all fired,
source restored byte-identical:

| mutation | what it broke | result |
|---|---|---|
| `coverage` reverts to the default under a limit | a limited search claims completeness | fail: `expected 1 to be 4` |
| `assistReason` tests `indexed` before `entries` | an empty type is told its entries are unsearchable | fail at unit and CLI level |
| the SQL `ESCAPE` clause removed | the escape pair no longer holds | fail |
| the JS pattern escaping removed | `_` reaches SQLite as a wildcard | fail: the only test that isolates the hazard |
| `countSearchMatches` ignores the type | the total counts another type's matches | fail |
| `searchScope` counts entries instead of indexed | every type reports itself searchable | fail |
| the two `searchScope` counts swapped | the two numbers mean each other | fail |
| `MIN_TERM_LENGTH` lowered to 1 | a two-character query is answered instead of refused | fail |
| the assist made present on a hit | absence stops signalling a zero result | fail |
| the assist rendered but not emitted | the explanation never reaches stdout | fail |
| the value ordering reversed | the limit keeps the rarest values | fail |
| the empty-terms guard removed | an empty disjunction reaches the driver | fail |

**One test was found to be asserting nothing, and that is the most useful result here.** The first
`___` test passed with the escape **removed**, because the fixture's property values (`cargo test`,
`pnpm build`) contain no underscore -- there was no value for the wildcard to over-match, so the test
asserted the absence of a hazard it could not produce. A second, isolating mutation (JS escaping
removed, SQL clause kept) exposed it. The fixture gained a value containing an underscore
(`npm_run_build`), and a companion test now asserts the other half -- that escaping `_` does not make
it unmatchable, which a "fix" that simply broke underscore queries would otherwise pass.

**Decision**    Ship the search, with the assist reporting **why** there is nothing rather than only
                what else might be said: an exhaustive `reason` of `type-empty`, `nothing-indexed` or
                `no-match`, the two counts behind it, and the property values that actually occur and
                contain a query term, each with the number of entries carrying it. On this corpus the
                first two reasons are the ones that fire, and the third is the rare case.

                **Counted, never inferred, for anything a caller reads as a total.** The match count
                is a real `COUNT(*)`, so a limited result states its denominator; the suggested values
                carry real counts, so a suggestion cannot name a value no entry holds; and the two
                scope counts are read from the store rather than derived from each other.

                **The vocabulary pass is not ported**, on the measurement in Q3: the metric misses
                transpositions entirely at its own floor, the floor has no provenance, and the corpus
                the pass would run over is one type deep. The property-value pass is the same idea
                aimed at the space ascend has, and it is the only one of the two with a real caller
                here -- `verification_run` and `tool_denial` are exactly the types the text index
                cannot reach and exactly the types whose properties are richest (542 and 846 distinct
                text values, against `user_correction`'s 39).

                **The assist is advisory and its rendering says so**, opening with `no matches.` and
                naming each value as something a *search does not cover* rather than as a result. A
                suggestion a skimming caller counts as a hit would be this block's one way to do harm.

**Confidence**  High for Q2 and for the mechanism of Q1: the coverage defect was reproduced by the
                real binary and the numbers re-derived from an independent `COUNT`, and every mutation
                above fired.

                High that **four of the five populated types in this corpus are unsearchable** -- that
                is a direct reading of the store, and the assist's central claim rests on it.

                **Medium for Q1's generality and for Q3, and the limitations are stated rather than
                implied.** n = 1, and this corpus is derived rather than earned: it was backfilled, so
                4 of its 9 types have 0 entries and all 1,491 entries share one `recorded_at`. The
                shape that matters -- most types carrying no evidence text -- is a consequence of how
                the corpus was built, and **a corpus earned from live workflows may set
                `evidence_text` far more often**, which would move the common reason from
                `nothing-indexed` to `no-match` and make the property-value pass rarer. The assist
                handles either, but the balance measured here is not evidence about the other.

                `ASSIST_VALUE_LIMIT = 5` is **a judgement, not a measurement**, and is commented as
                one. Measured, real queries produce one to three suggestions here, so the bound trims
                nothing -- but that is a fact about this corpus, not a defence of the number.

                Unverified: the `json_each` scan at scale (0.73 ms on 1,491 entries; the largest type
                measured holds 486), since a type with six figures of entries and many properties was
                never constructed; the assist's value for a caller who is not a model; and the
                behaviour of `--limit` above the true match count, which is exercised at 50 against a
                two-row result but never against a corpus large enough for the count to be the
                expensive part.
