# EV-57: a line-traced outcome and a within-repo control for n8n's process changes

**Status**      Measured 2026-10-13 for `asc-cw3c`. Predictions were in the bead's notes before the
                tracing script was written; not sealed by sha256. Read-only on the clone: `git log`,
                `git diff` and `git blame`, nothing built, run or modified.

**Question**    `EV-56` left two faults: an outcome that was near saturated and rose with commit
                volume, and a before/after at one date that random dates reproduce. Does tracing
                fixes to the lines they rewrite give a usable outcome, and does a control inside
                the repo let a process change be graded?

## Method

- **Outcome.** For each of the 7,736 `fix` commits since 2022 with 30 files or fewer, the lines it
  deletes or rewrites in source files are blamed at its parent (`git blame -w`), which names the
  commit that introduced each line. A (commit, file) pair is **fixed** when a fix commit rewrites
  one of its lines within 30 days; a commit is fixed when any of its pairs is. Renames are not
  followed.
- **Interventions.** The first creation of a hook, lint, test or agent-instruction file in each
  directory. Kept only when the directory is not the root (a root file covers everything, so
  nothing is left to compare with) and the directory already had commits 90 days earlier (so the
  file is a change to an existing area, not a config arriving with a new package).
- **Control.** Covered pairs are those under the directory; the control is every other pair. The
  estimate is the difference in differences: the change in the covered rate from the 90 days
  before to the 90 days after, minus the same change in the control. At least 20 pairs are needed
  in each of the four cells.
- **Noise band.** The same estimate at 300 random dates for the same directory (seed 20261013);
  the central 90% is the band.

## Predictions

P1 the yearly rate's max over min is at most 1.25 (the old measure: 1.48). P2 between 5% and 25%
of eligible commits are fixed. P3 feat is highest, then refactor, then chore. P4 at least 20
gradeable interventions, at least 5 of them agent instructions. P5 at most 15% fall outside their
band, and the agent-instruction estimates do not share a sign.

## Decision

**The outcome is now usable. One repo does not hold enough gradeable experiments.**

1. **The line-traced outcome is a clear improvement.** It is far from saturated (16.9% against
   44.4%) and separates change types much more sharply: feat commits are fixed 2.7 times as often
   as chore commits, where the old measure gave 1.5.
2. **It still drifts upward over time, and I do not know why.** Three candidates, none tested:
   more commits being labelled `fix` in later years, so more fixes are seen; a real rise; or the
   2026 surge in agent-marked work. The control is what protects an estimate from this, since a
   repo-wide drift moves both arms.
3. **The control works as a design and starves for material.** Of 205 scoped process files, 116
   arrived with their directory, 70 fall outside measurable dates, 4 are at the root and 2 have
   too few pairs. Thirteen remain.
4. **Thirteen is an anecdote under `MIN_N`.** Seven estimates are negative and six positive. The
   three outside their band: a 2022 lint config in `packages/workflow` (worse, from a before-rate
   of 0 in 43 pairs), a 2024 lint config in `cypress` (better: 9.5% to 1.8% while the rest of the
   repo rose), and a 2026 agent file in `packages/@n8n/db` (better, past its band by 0.0002).
5. **No effect of agent instruction files is shown.** Five directories, three estimates negative
   and two positive, four inside their bands. The largest, `packages/frontend` with 1,532 and
   2,190 pairs, moved by -0.4 points.

Next: pool the same pipeline across repos, so the unit becomes the intervention and there are
enough of them. vscode cannot use this fix definition as it stands: 3,386 of its 61,740 commits
since 2022 carry a conventional type, so "fix" has to come from issue references or wording.

## Confidence

**High** for the counts and the funnel. **Medium** for the outcome as a measure of rework: blame
attributes a rewritten line to its last author, which is the known weakness of this method, and a
fix that only adds lines is invisible. **Low** for any single estimate, for the reason in point 4.
One repo.
