# EV-56: does n8n's git history hold enough to mine for betterment priors?

**Status**      Measured 2026-10-13 for `asc-n8sv`, the first pass of `asc-m3sb`. Predictions were in
                the bead's notes before any n8n count was run; not sealed by sha256. Two blocks
                (`power_since_2022` and the 2026 agent tables) were added after the first run was
                read and are exploratory.

**Question**    Git only, one repo: are there enough dated process changes, enough linking, and
                enough outcome signal to treat the history as a set of natural experiments?

## Method

`git log --no-merges --name-status --no-renames` over `~/temp/enterprise-apps/n8n`, read with git
and a scratch script; nothing in the clone was built or run.

- **Linking and typing:** a PR number at the end of the subject; a conventional-commit type.
- **Reverts:** a `revert` type or a "This reverts commit" body, resolved to the commit reverted.
- **Fix-after rate:** a commit is followed by a fix when a later `fix` commit within 14 days
  touches one of its source files. Lockfiles, manifests, changelogs, docs and data files are not
  source; commits over 30 files and the last 14 days are not eligible. A proxy for rework, not a
  traced cause.
- **Process changes:** commits touching CI workflows, hook config, lint config, test config, PR or
  issue templates, CODEOWNERS or agent instruction files, and the subset that create such a file.
- **Agent-marked commits:** a co-author trailer or author naming an AI agent.
- **Power:** the fix-after rate in the 90 days after a process-file creation minus the 90 days
  before, against the same difference at 500 random dates (seed 20261013).

## Predictions

P1 at least 90% of commits since 2022 carry a PR number. P2 at least 85% carry a type. P3 reverts
are under 1% and at least 20 resolve. P4 at least 50 commits create a process file and 20 have 90
days both sides. P5 feat commits are followed by a fix more often than refactor and chore, and the
overall rate is between 15% and 40%. P6 at least 200 agent-marked commits. P7 at least 80% of
creations fall inside the central 90% of the random-date differences.

## Decision

**The material is there. The outcome measure is not good enough yet, and one date in one repo
cannot be graded.**

1. **Linking, typing and volume are not the problem.** Nine commits in ten since 2022 can be tied
   to a PR and typed, and there are hundreds of dated process changes.
2. **The fix-after rate is too blunt.** It is undefined before 2022, when subjects carried no
   type. At 44% it is close to saturated, and it rises with commit volume (33.5% in 2022 on 1,160
   eligible commits, 49.5% in 2026 on 6,906), so a busier repo looks worse for no other reason.
3. **Single-date before and after is noise.** Only 6 of 277 creations fall outside the band that
   random dates produce, fewer than the 28 chance alone would give. Natural experiments need a
   control inside the repo (files the change covered against files it did not) or pooling across
   repos, and a metric scoped to the change.
4. **n8n is an agent-era repo more than a long-history one.** A third of all its commits are from
   2026, and agent-marked commits rose from 6.6% of the month in January 2026 to 51.3% in August.
   `CLAUDE.md` first appears on 2025-08-14 and 56 commits create agent instruction files.
5. **Agent-marked and other commits do not differ consistently** on fix-after rate within 2026:
   higher in five months, lower in four. Unmarked agent work is counted as other.
6. **"Creation" over-counts interventions.** Many are a config file arriving with a new package,
   not a rule change. A real intervention list needs the diff, not the path.

Next, in `asc-m3sb`: replace the outcome proxy with one scoped to the change and insensitive to
volume, and build the within-repo control, before running any other repo.

**High** for the counts. **Medium** for the agent share: it counts marked commits only. **Low**
for anything the fix-after rate implies about quality, for the reasons in point 2. One repo.
