# EV-72: is a commit-level analysis more powerful than the segment-level one, for the commit-risk nudge?

**Status**      Measured 2026-10-15 for `asc-09wx.12`. **No predictions were registered before this
                ran, and it is not a prediction test** — the question was put to the owner as the
                arm's last open design choice and answered *measure it*, so this measurement was
                commissioned rather than hypothesised. `EV-71` is not revised by it: that record's
                own widened `any` cell is reproduced exactly below, as the cross-check that makes
                the two levels comparable at all.

**Question**    `EV-71` measured the design effect at the level the hook randomizes — the
                compaction segment (`asc-6ola.4`: *unit = segment x intervention*) — and had to
                collapse each segment's commit triggers to one 0/1, because `clusterDesign` accepts
                only a binary outcome. Four rules were reported and the choice left open, worth 139
                days. A fifth option was never measured: **do not collapse at all.** Analyse at the
                commit level, intent-to-treat, with the variance clustered. That is the standard
                choice for cluster randomization and is usually *more* efficient than cluster-level
                analysis, because collapsing to the cluster discards the within-cluster information.
                The owner chose `any` at the segment level and asked for this option to be measured
                before the arm is registered on it.

## Method

Read-only. The same join (`out/join.jsonl`), the same eight repositories and the same traces
`rho.py` reads; nothing was copied into the repo.

- **`cost/commits.py`** emits every `(unit, commit)` observation behind `EV-71`'s 394 units, using
  the widened, source-bearing, window-closed predicate **copied verbatim from `rho.py`** so that the
  two levels describe one population rather than two. It also re-derives the segment-level `any`
  from those same rows, which is what the cross-check below compares against.
- **`cost/commits.mjs`** runs the package's own `clusterDesign` and `powerPlan`
  (`packages/analysis/dist/index.js`). No second implementation of either.
- **Two cluster keys are run, because the answer is entirely in that choice.** `cluster=unit` (the
  segment) is the design-based key for a commit-level ITT: it clusters at the level the hook
  randomizes. `cluster=session` is a superset of it. The segment-level analysis has no such choice
  — every unit *is* a cluster, so the only admissible key there is the session, and running it at
  `cluster=unit` would make every cluster a singleton.
- **One clock.** `perDay` is units accrued per active day of the *same* working period, so both
  levels are reported on `EV-71`'s 48 days. The 41 days the commit observations actually landed on
  is reported beside it as a sensitivity and not as the basis — using it for the commit rows and 48
  for the segment row would move 82 to 70 and manufacture a win for the wrong analysis.

**The bar:** a commit-level design whose effective N is materially above the segment-level `any`
cell's 85.544, at the *same* cluster key. A gain that exists only under a different key is not a
gain, it is a different assumption.

## Measurement

The population, before either design is computed:

```
units 394   units_any_rate 0.78173
commit_observations 764   distinct_commits 763   commit_rate 0.68455
commits_per_unit 1.94     commit_active_days 41     sessions 24
cross_session_commits 1
```

**1.94, not 5.4.** `EV-71` reports 5.38 triggers per unit, but that count is over every commit
trigger; only 1.94 of them carry a gradeable outcome once the widened source-bearing, window-closed
predicate is applied. So the commit level is not five times the segment level. It is not twice it.

The cross-check, and it lands exactly:

## What the measurement says

**The commit-level analysis is not more powerful. It is less.** Clustered at the session it costs 82
active days against the segment-level `any` cell's 70, with an effective N of 104.5 against 85.5 — the
higher effective N does not survive division by the accrual rate, because a session's commits
are far more skewed than its segments. `sizeFactor` at commit level is 81.296, at segment level
38.609, and `largestCluster` goes 63 to 126. `EV-71`'s own lesson repeats here: *a falling rho is
not a falling design effect*. `rho` falls again, 0.09588 to 0.07859, and the design still gets
dearer.

The whole table, with the term that moves:

```
                                 n  clusters largest  rho       sizeFactor  deff      effN
segment any    cluster=session 394    24      63    0.09588     38.609    4.60581   85.544
commit         cluster=unit    764   394       9    0.19245      2.804    1.34712  567.134
commit         cluster=session 764    24     126    0.07859     81.296    7.31061  104.506
```

**The 15-day figure is the cluster key, not the analysis.** Dropping to `cluster=unit` cuts the
design effect 5.4x and the horizon 82 days to 15. That cut is not extra information; it is the
between-segment-within-session correlation being assumed away. Its size is measurable and it is not
small: holding the observations fixed and changing only the key takes `deff` from 1.347 to 7.311,
and `EV-71` had already measured the same quantity from the other side at the segment level — `rho`
0.09588 *between segments sharing a session*. Two segments in one session are not independent
draws, and the segment key is the one key that cannot see it. This is the failure mode
`design-effect.ts` exists for, arriving through a door it does not close: the estimator refuses a
non-binary outcome and refuses a negative rho, but the cluster key is the caller's and it never
questions it.

**The comparison is only fair on one key, and on that key the segment-level analysis wins.** The
honest reading is not "15 days is available if we accept a risk" — it is that the commit level was
the one place a key could be chosen, and choosing it per analysis is how two surfaces come to
disagree.

## Decision

**The arm stays at the segment level under `any`**, at **70 active days** for a 30% reduction and 27
for a 50% reduction, on a baseline of 0.78173 and 8.2083 units per active day. The commit-level
alternative is closed as a lever rather than left open: measured on the cluster key consistent with
`EV-71`, it is 12 days worse, and the reading that makes it look 5.5x better requires the key
`EV-71` did not use.

`asc-09wx.12` is now registerable on units, baseline, rule and design effect that were all measured
rather than assumed. The one number still missing is the ceiling — the 489-unit population is not
computable until the 95 open 7-day windows close, late October, and `EV-71` explains why imputing
them is worse than waiting.

## Confidence

**High** in the arithmetic and in the reproduction: the segment-level cell matches `EV-71` to every
published digit, and the day counts here were re-derived independently of the harness that printed
them — `deff = 1 + (sizeFactor - 1) * rho` closes on all three rows (4.60581 from 38.609 and
0.09588; 1.34712 from 2.804 and 0.19245; 7.31061 from 81.296 and 0.07859), and the `@30%` column is
the required units over `perDay`, checked by hand.

**High** that the 15-day cell is not a finding. It is one key against another on identical
observations, and the excess it removes is a measured quantity on this corpus, not a hypothetical
one.

**Moderate** in the day figures as *schedule*, unchanged from `EV-71`: they scale linearly in a
design effect measured on the population available today, and the population will grow when the 95
windows close. The direction of that change is not known — `EV-71`'s three imputations all gave
*worse* designs, and those were labelled artifacts rather than estimates.

**Known weakness, carried from `EV-71` and not repaired here:** the join is 80% the weak route
(`[branch sha]` on 369 triggers, a +/-1 s window on 1,570). This measurement inherits it, and the
observation count is low enough that a mis-attributed commit is worth more here than it was at the
segment level — 764 observations against 394 units, with 1.94 per unit, so one moved commit moves
two cells.
