# EV-73: does a rebuilt commit map change any published figure, and can the 489-unit ceiling be measured yet?

**Status**      Measured 2026-10-15/16 for `asc-09wx.12` and `asc-lspj`. No predictions were registered,
                and like `EV-72` this was commissioned rather than hypothesised — by `EV-71`'s own two
                open items, *"regenerate the `ev63` numstat before any re-run"* and *"re-measure at the
                489-unit ceiling once the 95 windows close, late October"*. It revises nothing: every
                figure it re-derives lands on the digit its predecessor published.

**Question**    `EV-71` read a commit map assembled from `EV-63`'s `numstat.log` snapshots, which stop
                at 2026-10-13T06:20:34Z, plus a dump made during the same scoping for the four
                repositories `EV-63` did not trace. Two things it could not say: whether regenerating
                that map from the repositories changes anything measured, and whether the 489-unit
                ceiling — the population carrying a source-bearing commit at any tracing price — is
                measurable yet. `EV-71` left it censored rather than imputed and put the answer in
                *"mid- to late October"*, which is now.

## Method

Read-only against the eight repositories and the traces. `spike/rework-design-effect/cost/numstat.py`
re-dumps a repository's history in the format the harnesses parse; `.../delta.py` lists the
source-bearing commits no earlier run traced; `.../remeasure.py` runs the whole measurement over one
map and one clock; `design.mjs` and `commits.mjs` supply the package's own `clusterDesign` and
`powerPlan`. Nothing was copied into the repo.

**Two details of the dump format are not free choices, and both were recovered by reproducing the
existing files rather than by reading them.** The `\n` ending the format string is what puts the blank
line before the numstat block rather than after it. The other is the timestamp field, and it is not
settled in the existing data at all: **`EV-63`'s four snapshots record `%at`, the author date, and the
four grizzly dumps record `%ct`, the committer date.** ascend's `1f765ce3e4d1` holds 1789713547 in
`EV-63`'s snapshot, which is its `%at`; its `%ct` is 1789713993. grizzly-exp's `29095b9aa6dc` holds
1791232802 in the session's dump, which is its `%ct`; its `%at` is 1791232792. **130 commits across
the eight disagree, by up to 19.7 hours**, and every window test in `EV-71` —
`0 < rw_at - c['at'] <= 7*DAY` — subtracts two clocks for half the corpus. Both fields are dumped and
the field is a parameter, so the cost of having mixed them is measured below rather than argued.

## Decision

**Both of `EV-71`'s open items are closed, and neither moved a number.** The stale snapshot was not
affecting any published figure: 2,158 additional rework pairs flipped no unit's outcome, and the two
extra days of commits added no unit to the ceiling. The `%at`/`%ct` split is immaterial at this
resolution. The arm stands as `EV-72` registered it — `any` at the segment level, 70 active days, on
baseline 0.78173 and 8.2083 units per active day. Nothing needs re-registering.

**The ceiling stays unmeasurable, and now has a date.** The 95 open windows shut between 2026-10-15
and 2026-10-22; a re-measurement on **2026-10-22** sees 483 of 489. Re-dumping the map cannot bring
that forward, because the constraint is elapsed time and not data — which is the one thing a
regeneration pass is in a position to prove and this one did.

**`extra.numstat.log` is retired by the rebuild.** It existed only to patch the traced repositories'
post-snapshot commits, and every one of its commits is now in the map. It is left on disk as `EV-71`'s
artifact; nothing reads it.

## Confidence

**High** in the rebuild: all eight existing dumps are strict subsets of it, and it reproduces each of
them exactly on the overlap when compared against the field that dump was written with. The funnel
reproduces `EV-71`'s published 489 / 394 / 95 / 363 to the unit, and the outcome table reproduces
every published digit while the input it is computed from has grown by 2,158 pairs.

**High** that the clock split is immaterial here, because both fields were run end to end and agree on
every cell. 130 commits out of 17,822 disagree, and none of them moves a unit.

**Known weakness, and it is inherited rather than measured.** The legacy `t/` traces were written
under whichever clock existed when they were made, and cannot be re-derived; the `t2/D.*` traces were
written against the map being measured. So the pair set is not *purely* single-clock. Bounding it
properly needs a full re-trace under one field — 8,238 commits per side, against the 2,198 the delta
needs — and that was not run. What was run is the both-fields comparison, which agrees on every cell
and therefore says the residual is smaller than this measurement can resolve. `delta.py`'s own
`already()` set is what makes the union complete, so coverage is not in doubt; only the clock on half
of it is.

**One discrepancy with `EV-71`'s own output, stated so a later reader is not misled.** `out/measure.json`
reports `censored_window_open: 96` where `EV-71`'s prose says 95. They are two definitions: that file
counts a unit as censored if *any* of its source-bearing commits has an open window, while 489 = 394 +
95 requires "no commit closed". The prose figure and this record use the second.

**Not measured at all:** the design effect at the ceiling. It is still censored, for the reason
`EV-71` gave — `clusterDesign` accepts only 0/1, and imputing the 95 would inject between-cluster
variance and no within-cluster variance, which is the quantity `rho` estimates.
