# EV-20: at what threshold does near-duplicate collapse merge entries that are NOT saying the same thing?

> **PRE-REGISTRATION.** Everything above the `## Measurement` rule was written and committed
> BEFORE any collapse was run, as `asc-yce` requires ("Name the question BEFORE running").
> The predictions are numbered so that each can be marked hit or missed rather than reinterpreted.
> Commit history is the receipt.
>
> What WAS looked at first, and why that is not peeking: the corpus's **shape** — how many entries
> carry prose, how long it is, how many distinct values the categorical types take. Those numbers
> appear in Method below. They are needed to choose a surface and a sample size at all, and none of
> them is a similarity, a group, or a merge. No `collapseNearDuplicates` call had been made against
> this store when this file was committed.

> **Project identifiers are redacted.** This repository is public and the corpus spans twelve
> working directories, only one of which (`ascend`) is this repo. Private project names are
> replaced with stable pseudonyms; the same pseudonym always means the same project.

**Question**    `asc-yce` asks for near-duplicate collapse over `evidence_text` so that a reader
                sees "one representative and a count" instead of forty rows saying one thing, and
                it asks for one number in particular: **the FALSE-MERGE RATE at the chosen
                threshold.** A false merge is the expensive failure and the invisible one — a
                missed merge leaves two rows where one would do and a reader notices; a false merge
                destroys a distinction and leaves no trace that there was one.

                So: **at what Jaccard threshold does this corpus start merging entries that, read
                by hand, are making different claims — and what is the false-merge rate at the
                threshold we ship?**

                A second question the bead does not ask but the corpus forces: the bead's own
                motivating example is "38 near-identical denials of the same command". Those
                entries exist (`tool_denial`, N=564). **They carry no prose at all.** Whether the
                instrument can reach the data that motivated it is therefore also measured here,
                and it is the arm most likely to lose.

## Method

**The corpus.** `asc export` over the real store, 1,795 entries across 10 types, unmodified.

Text coverage, measured before choosing a surface:

| surface | entries | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| `evidence_text` non-empty | 67 | 3.7% |
| any prose (`evidence_text`, `decision.rationale`, `note.text`, `decision.chosen`) | 97 | 5.4% |
| no prose of any kind | 1,698 | 94.6% |

Prose length on the 97: min 7 words, p25 80, median 136, p75 186, max 1,047. None is under 3
words, so none is too short to shingle.

The three types that dominate the corpus by count — `tool_denial` (564), `context_compaction`
(527), `verification_run` (502) — are machine-derived by `asc ingest` and have no text property in
their schemas. `tool_denial` carries 14 distinct `(tool_name, denial_kind)` pairs across its 564
entries; its largest single pair covers 239.

**Arms.** All three run `collapseNearDuplicates` from `packages/analysis/src/neardup.ts`, seeded,
128 permutations, 32 bands.

- **Arm A — trigrams over prose (the candidate to ship).** All 97 prose entries, `shingleSize: 3`,
  threshold swept over 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9.
- **Arm B — bag of words over prose (the rival).** Same 97 entries, `shingleSize: 1`, same sweep.
  Order-free, so it should merge more; the question is whether the extra merges are right.
- **Arm C — the derived types (the surface the bead's example lives on).** `tool_denial`, N=564,
  with each entry's categorical properties joined into a pseudo-document.

**Adjudication.** At each arm's chosen threshold, EVERY merged pair is read by hand and marked
`same` or `different`. Same = the two entries make the same claim about the same thing; a
paraphrase counts as same. Different = they make different claims, even if they share subject or
vocabulary. **False-merge rate = pairs marked `different` / pairs judged.** Chained groups are
adjudicated on their weakest pair, because that is the pair the group asserts and the index never
proposed.

## Predictions

1. **Arm A at 0.8 finds fewer than 5 groups.** 97 hand-written entries spanning ten types do not
   repeat each other's trigrams.
2. **Arm A's false-merge rate at 0.8 is 0.00.** Eighty per cent trigram overlap across a
   136-word median document is near-verbatim; there should be nothing to get wrong.
3. **Arm A at 0.5 still finds fewer than 15 groups.** The instrument will be correct and mostly
   idle: this corpus's prose is not where the repetition is.
4. **Arm B merges strictly more pairs than Arm A at every threshold, and at least one of the extra
   merges is false** — two entries sharing vocabulary while making different claims. This is the
   arm predicted to lose, and the prediction is that it loses on false merges, not on volume.
5. **Arm C degenerates.** With 14 distinct tuples over 564 entries, every group will be an exact
   duplicate group: `chainedGroups` = 0 and every group's `minSimilarity` = 1.0, at every
   threshold. Near-duplicate collapse will have reproduced `GROUP BY`, more slowly.
6. **The largest prose group has 4 members or fewer.** The bead's "forty rows saying the same
   thing" is real, but it is in the derived types, and they carry no text.

**Decision rule, fixed in advance.** Ship `threshold` default 0.8 with `shingleSize` 3 if
prediction 2 holds. If it does not, ship the LOWEST swept threshold whose measured false-merge rate
is 0.00, and record the rate at 0.8 as the reason. If no swept threshold reaches 0.00, ship none
and say the technique is not safe on this corpus.
