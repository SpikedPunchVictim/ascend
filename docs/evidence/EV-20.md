# EV-20: at what threshold does near-duplicate collapse merge entries that are NOT saying the same thing?

> **PRE-REGISTRATION.** Everything above the `## Measurement` rule was written and committed
> BEFORE any collapse was run, as `asc-yce` requires ("Name the question BEFORE running").
> The predictions are numbered so that each can be marked hit or missed rather than reinterpreted.
> Commit history is the receipt.
>
> What WAS looked at first, and why that is not peeking: the corpus's **shape** — how many entries
> carry prose, how long it is, how many distinct values the categorical types take. Those numbers
> appear in Method below. They are needed to choose a surface and a sample size at all, and none of
> them is a similarity, a group, or a merge. No `collapseNearDuplicates` call had been made against
> this store when this file was committed.

> **Project identifiers are redacted.** This repository is public and the corpus spans twelve
> working directories, only one of which (`ascend`) is this repo. Private project names are
> replaced with stable pseudonyms; the same pseudonym always means the same project.

**Question**    `asc-yce` asks for near-duplicate collapse over `evidence_text` so that a reader
                sees "one representative and a count" instead of forty rows saying one thing, and
                it asks for one number in particular: **the FALSE-MERGE RATE at the chosen
                threshold.** A false merge is the expensive failure and the invisible one — a
                missed merge leaves two rows where one would do and a reader notices; a false merge
                destroys a distinction and leaves no trace that there was one.

                So: **at what Jaccard threshold does this corpus start merging entries that, read
                by hand, are making different claims — and what is the false-merge rate at the
                threshold we ship?**

                A second question the bead does not ask but the corpus forces: the bead's own
                motivating example is "38 near-identical denials of the same command". Those
                entries exist (`tool_denial`, N=564). **They carry no prose at all.** Whether the
                instrument can reach the data that motivated it is therefore also measured here,
                and it is the arm most likely to lose.

## Method

**The corpus.** `asc export` over the real store, 1,795 entries across 10 types, unmodified.

Text coverage, measured before choosing a surface:

| surface | entries | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| `evidence_text` non-empty | 67 | 3.7% |
| any prose (`evidence_text`, `decision.rationale`, `note.text`, `decision.chosen`) | 97 | 5.4% |
| no prose of any kind | 1,698 | 94.6% |

Prose length on the 97: min 7 words, p25 80, median 136, p75 186, max 1,047. None is under 3
words, so none is too short to shingle.

The three types that dominate the corpus by count — `tool_denial` (564), `context_compaction`
(527), `verification_run` (502) — are machine-derived by `asc ingest` and have no text property in
their schemas. `tool_denial` carries 14 distinct `(tool_name, denial_kind)` pairs across its 564
entries; its largest single pair covers 239.

**Arms.** All three run `collapseNearDuplicates` from `packages/analysis/src/neardup.ts`, seeded,
128 permutations, 32 bands.

- **Arm A — trigrams over prose (the candidate to ship).** All 97 prose entries, `shingleSize: 3`,
  threshold swept over 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9.
- **Arm B — bag of words over prose (the rival).** Same 97 entries, `shingleSize: 1`, same sweep.
  Order-free, so it should merge more; the question is whether the extra merges are right.
- **Arm C — the derived types (the surface the bead's example lives on).** `tool_denial`, N=564,
  with each entry's categorical properties joined into a pseudo-document.

**Adjudication.** At each arm's chosen threshold, EVERY merged pair is read by hand and marked
`same` or `different`. Same = the two entries make the same claim about the same thing; a
paraphrase counts as same. Different = they make different claims, even if they share subject or
vocabulary. **False-merge rate = pairs marked `different` / pairs judged.** Chained groups are
adjudicated on their weakest pair, because that is the pair the group asserts and the index never
proposed.

## Predictions

1. **Arm A at 0.8 finds fewer than 5 groups.** 97 hand-written entries spanning ten types do not
   repeat each other's trigrams.
2. **Arm A's false-merge rate at 0.8 is 0.00.** Eighty per cent trigram overlap across a
   136-word median document is near-verbatim; there should be nothing to get wrong.
3. **Arm A at 0.5 still finds fewer than 15 groups.** The instrument will be correct and mostly
   idle: this corpus's prose is not where the repetition is.
4. **Arm B merges strictly more pairs than Arm A at every threshold, and at least one of the extra
   merges is false** — two entries sharing vocabulary while making different claims. This is the
   arm predicted to lose, and the prediction is that it loses on false merges, not on volume.
5. **Arm C degenerates.** With 14 distinct tuples over 564 entries, every group will be an exact
   duplicate group: `chainedGroups` = 0 and every group's `minSimilarity` = 1.0, at every
   threshold. Near-duplicate collapse will have reproduced `GROUP BY`, more slowly.
6. **The largest prose group has 4 members or fewer.** The bead's "forty rows saying the same
   thing" is real, but it is in the derived types, and they carry no text.

**Decision rule, fixed in advance.** Ship `threshold` default 0.8 with `shingleSize` 3 if
prediction 2 holds. If it does not, ship the LOWEST swept threshold whose measured false-merge rate
is 0.00, and record the rate at 0.8 as the reason. If no swept threshold reaches 0.00, ship none
and say the technique is not safe on this corpus.

### Hand adjudication of the prose arms

Every merged pair in Arm A (3 pairs at 0.40) and Arm B (6 pairs at 0.50, 28 at 0.40) was read.
**Every one is the same finding.** All merged entries are `user_correction`, and all of them open
with an identical 43-word preamble emitted by Claude Code's AskUserQuestion tool — "The user wants
to clarify these questions… Start by asking them what they would like to clarify. Questions
asked:" — after which they ask entirely unrelated things: how to fund a testnet wallet, how `int
apply` should hard-delete accounts, where to start on issue #11, how to fix a Windows
handle-pinning bug in `FileIdentity`, how deep a New Relic measurement should go.

By the adjudication rule fixed above ("same = the two entries make the same claim about the same
thing"), these make **different** claims and share a template.

| arm | threshold | pairs judged | false | false-merge rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A — trigram | 0.40 | 3 | 3 | **1.000000** |
| A — trigram | 0.50 … 0.90 | 0 | — | **no denominator — omitted** |
| B — bag of words | 0.40 | 28 | 28 | **1.000000** |
| B — bag of words | 0.50 | 6 | 6 | **1.000000** |
| B — bag of words | 0.60 … 0.90 | 0 | — | **no denominator — omitted** |

The rate at Arm A / 0.80 — the number this record set out to produce — **does not exist**. Zero
pairs merged, so there is no denominator. It is omitted here rather than written as `0.00`, which
would be the same claim as "we merged nothing wrongly" and is not what was measured.

## Predictions: hit or missed

1. **Arm A at 0.8 finds fewer than 5 groups** — **HIT**, 0 groups. Vacuously: it finds nothing at
   all from 0.50 up.
2. **Arm A's false-merge rate at 0.8 is 0.00** — **NOT EVALUABLE.** No pairs merged, so the rate
   has no denominator. Not a hit. Recording it as a hit would be writing `0` for unknown.
3. **Arm A at 0.5 still finds fewer than 15 groups** — **HIT**, 0 groups.
4. **Arm B merges strictly more pairs than Arm A at every threshold, and at least one extra merge
   is false** — **MISSED on the first clause, HIT on the second.** Arm B merges more at 0.40
   (16 vs 3) and 0.50 (4 vs 0), but ties at 0 from 0.60 up, so "at every threshold" is false. Every
   extra merge, not just one, is false.
5. **Arm C degenerates to exact-duplicate groups: `chainedGroups` 0 and every `minSimilarity`
   1.0** — **MISSED, and the reasoning error is identifiable.** The prediction assumed the
   pseudo-document was a two-value tuple taking only Jaccard 0 or 1. The harness joins three
   properties and tokenizes them, so the documents run to several tokens and Jaccard takes many
   fractional values. What actually happened is the opposite of degenerate: at 0.40–0.60 a single
   chained group swallows **562 of 564 entries**, weakest pair sharing one token in six
   (`minSimilarity` 0.166667), for a false-merge rate of **0.723989**.
6. **The largest prose group has 4 members or fewer** — **HIT for Arm A** (3), **MISSED for Arm B**
   (8 at threshold 0.40).

Two hits, one partial, two misses, one question that could not be answered. The prediction that
failed hardest — 5 — is the one that produced the only usable threshold curve in the record.

## Decision

**The pre-registered decision rule did not survive contact with the data, and the reason is worth
stating rather than papering over.** It read: ship 0.8 if prediction 2 holds; otherwise ship the
lowest swept threshold whose measured false-merge rate is 0.00. Prediction 2 turned out to be
neither true nor false — it has no denominator — so the rule's first branch does not fire and its
second branch presupposes a measured rate on a surface that produced no merges to measure. The rule
assumed the prose arm would merge something. It did not.

So the decision below is a judgement made after seeing the data, and is labelled as one.

**Raise the module's default `threshold` from 0.8 to 0.9.** Two independent readings agree, which
is the strongest position available here:

- It is what the rule's second branch yields on the only arm with a defined false-merge rate: on
  Arm C, 0.90 is the lowest swept threshold measuring **0.000000**, and 0.80 measures
  **0.000930** — 28 wrong pairs out of 30,112.
- It costs nothing on the surface the default is actually for. Arm A merges **0 pairs at every
  threshold from 0.50 to 0.90**, so moving the default from 0.8 to 0.9 changes no prose outcome
  anywhere in this corpus. The change is free here and strictly safer where merges do occur.

**`shingleSize` stays 3.** Arm B is the losing arm and lost the way predicted: at 0.50 it merged 6
pairs that trigrams left alone, and all 6 are false. Order-free matching buys nothing on this
corpus except the template collisions.

**A negative result, said plainly: this corpus cannot test the thing the bead was written to
build.** `asc-yce` is motivated by "the same finding repeated forty times" and names `tool_denial`'s
repetition as the case. Those entries exist — 564 of them — and they carry no prose. The 97 entries
that do carry prose are hand-written, one-off, and near-duplicate-free: above a 0.5 trigram
threshold there is not one pair in the corpus to collapse. The instrument is correct and, on the
surface it was built for, idle. That is a real finding about this store, not a failure of the
module, and the module ships with it recorded.

**What the measurement bought that the question did not ask for.** The only false-merge curve in
the record came from the arm predicted to be a formality, and the only merges on the prose arm were
all caused by 43 words of tool boilerplate that nobody had looked at. Both are filed.

## Confidence

**High on Arm C's curve.** Every pair in every group was adjudicated mechanically against
`(tool_name, denial_kind)` — 157,642 pairs at the low thresholds, no sampling, no judgement call.

**High on the prose arms' direction, low on their precision.** N=97 is above `MIN_N` (20,
`packages/analysis/src/proportion.ts:50`), but the 37 pairs actually judged come from a single
group of 8 built from a single defect in a single entry type. "Trigram similarity above 0.5 finds
nothing in this corpus's prose" is solid; "the false-merge rate at 0.8 would be low" is not
measured and is not claimed.

**The `evidence_text` coverage limit is the binding one.** 1,698 of 1,795 entries (94.6%) carry no
prose, by design. Any future claim about near-duplicate collapse on this store is a claim about
5.4% of it until that changes.
