# EV-62: can a type-free rework outcome stand in for typed fixes, on n8n?

**Status**      Measured 2026-10-13 for `asc-2tjl`. Design, bar and predictions were in the bead's
                description before any of the new trace was read (the trace itself was started
                first, in the background); not sealed by sha256. Nothing was added afterwards.

**Question**    `EV-61` could not test the size-and-churn rule on our own repositories because
                none of them types its commits, and `EV-57`'s outcome needs a `fix` label. Can an
                outcome that reads no labels reach the same conclusions where both can be computed?

## Method

n8n at `cea974e770027a0834e1e7be55fbfb6c23169b03`, read-only. `EV-57` traced the lines each `fix`
commit rewrites (31,884 rows). This adds the same trace for the 11,213 other non-merge commits
since 2022 with 30 files or fewer (82,264 rows), so every rewrite is known whatever the rewriting
commit is called.

A commit is positive when a later, different commit rewrites a line of one of its source files:

- `fixed_typed`, the reference: the rewriter is typed `fix`, within 30 days.
- `reworked_30`: any rewriter, within 30 days.
- `reworked_7`: any rewriter, within 7 days.
- `reworked_small_30`: the rewriter adds 20 source lines or fewer across 3 files or fewer.

Compared on 15,396 eligible commits (`EV-57` counted 15,398; this script counts a commit's files
slightly differently). Features use `EV-60`'s relative thresholds, taken from 2022 to 2025: 399
added lines and 11 changes in 30 days.

**The bar:** Cohen's kappa against the reference at least 0.4, and the reference's conclusions
reproduced: `size_top_decile` at least 1.3, `first_touch` below 1.0, `no_test_alongside` between
0.7 and 1.3. Churn is reported and not in the bar, since a busy file is rewritten more by
definition.

## Predictions

P1 `reworked_30` has a base rate between 35% and 50%. P2 it fails the bar on kappa. P3
`reworked_small_30` clears the bar with the highest kappa. P4 all three give size at least 1.3 and
first touch below 1.0. P5 churn's ratio is larger under `reworked_30` than under the reference. P6
at least 60% of small rewriters are typed `fix`, and under 45% of all rewriters. P7
`no_test_alongside` stays between 0.7 and 1.3 under all three.

## Decision

**A plain rework outcome stands in for typed fixes. The one built to look like a fix does not.**

1. **`reworked_30` and `reworked_7` both clear the bar** and reach every conclusion the typed
   outcome reached: size strongly up, churn up, first touch down, no test alongside flat.
2. **The ratios are attenuated, not changed.** Size is 2.54 against typed fixes, 2.07 against
   7-day rework and 1.84 against 30-day rework: ordinary iteration dilutes the outcome, more so
   over a longer window.
3. **Guessing a fix from its shape was the wrong idea.** Only 56% of small rewriters are typed
   `fix`, the outcome has the lowest agreement, and it manufactures an effect the reference does
   not have (`no_test_alongside` at 2.15).
4. **The fear that a rework outcome inflates churn did not show.** Churn's ratio is lower under
   rework than under typed fixes. That does not settle whether churn restates the outcome under
   EITHER definition, which `asc-09wx.2` still has to test.
5. **One disagreement to carry:** in 2026 alone, `no_test_alongside` reads 1.24 and 1.37 under the
   rework outcomes where the typed outcome reads 1.01.

For our own repositories, use `reworked_7` first: it tracks the reference's ratios more closely
and needs seven days of later history, where our transcripts span 49. Report `reworked_30` beside
it.

## Confidence

**High** for the counts. **Medium to high** that rework within a window can replace the typed
outcome for grading features in a repository like n8n. **Untested** on any second repository, and
on repositories whose commits are larger and whose files churn far more, which is what ours are
(`EV-61`). The reference is itself a proxy: agreement with it is not agreement with defects.
