# EV-22: can this corpus decide whether a stratified sample makes a model classify better?

**Status**      **NOT the measurement `asc-asq` asked for.** This record reports a *feasibility*
                determination and a *re-verification*, and the bead's question — does stratified
                sampling improve LLM classification accuracy over random — is **left open**. It is
                written down because the reason it cannot be run here is arithmetic, checkable, and
                names the corpus property a future attempt must go and find.

                This record is **not pre-registered**, and does not claim to be. A prediction *was*
                formed before measuring; it was then refuted by reading an existing record and by
                two direct probes, before any sweep was run. That sequence is reported in
                *The prediction that died* below, because a refuted prediction is the part of this
                worth keeping.

**Question**    `asc-asq`: *"Does stratified sampling actually improve LLM classification accuracy
                over random?"* with the decision rule *"if stratified shows no advantage, DROP the
                mode rather than carry it."*

                The bead's method is *"hand-label a full corpus slice as GROUND TRUTH… classify
                from a random sample and from a stratified sample of EQUAL SIZE."* One improvement
                on that method was available and taken: **the labels already exist.** `denial_kind`,
                `verdict` and `reversibility` are recorded properties, so ground truth is the corpus
                itself at full scale rather than a hand-labelled slice. Nothing else about the
                method changed.

                For sampling mode to affect *classification* at all, the mechanism has to be that
                the sample is what the model learns the category vocabulary from — n labelled
                examples in the prompt, then held-out items to classify. A sample that contains zero
                examples of a rare category cannot teach it, and recall on that category collapses.
                That mechanism is what makes the question well-posed, and it is also what makes it
                measurable only under a specific arithmetic condition, below.

## What was already settled before this record

EV-12 measured the *selection* half on a frozen 1,491-entry store over **200 draws per arm**:

| arm | draws missing ≥1 value | the singleton value absent in |
|---|---|---|
| `random` | **200 / 200 (100 %)** | **185 / 200 (92.5 %)** |
| `stratified` | **0 / 200 (0 %)** | **0 / 200 (0 %)** |

EV-12 also named its own limitation in terms that scope this record exactly: *"Whether a stratified
sample produces a better answer than a random one of the same size is a different question and is
not measured here — that is `asc-asq`… If stratified shows no advantage there, the mode is to be
dropped rather than carried. **Unproven.**"*

So the selection advantage was already banked at high confidence. What was open is only whether it
buys a better *answer*.

## The prediction that died

Before reading EV-12, one observed stratified draw at `--limit 20` on `tool_denial` by `denial_kind`
returned `{permission-rule: 9, user-rejected: 5, automode-unavailable: 5, automode-blocked: 1}`. The
rare category holds 36 of 564 entries — 6.4 %, and 6.4 % × 20 = 1.28. That is consistent with plain
proportional rounding, so the prediction formed was:

> **P2 (refuted).** Proportional allocation only guarantees a rare category appears while its share
> × n rounds to ≥ 1. Below that — n ≤ 6 here, where 36/564 × n < 0.5 — stratified rounds the rare
> stratum to **zero** and degrades to random, buying nothing exactly where it is needed most.

**P2 is false, and the single observed draw could not have told the difference** (0.511 and 1.277
both round to 1 under either rule). The allocator pays a **floor**: every stratum gets at least one
row. EV-12 had already measured it — a stratum of population 1 with an exact quota of **0.08 was
allocated 1 row** — and two probes against today's binary and the live store confirm the floor is
still paid:

```
$ asc explore decision --sample stratified --by reversibility --limit 20
stratified, by reversibility, seed "ascend": 4 strata, 0 unsampled
  costly  4 of 11
  irreversible  1 of 1          <- exact quota 1/64 x 20 = 0.31, allocated 1
  reversible  14 of 49
  not_measured  1 of 3

$ asc explore tool_denial --sample stratified --by denial_kind --limit 4
stratified, by denial_kind, seed "ascend": 4 strata, 0 unsampled
  automode-blocked  1 of 36     <- quota 0.26
  automode-unavailable  1 of 130
  permission-rule  1 of 242     <- quota 1.72, still 1: the floor dominates
  user-rejected  1 of 156

$ asc explore tool_denial --sample stratified --by denial_kind --limit 2
stratified, by denial_kind, seed "ascend": 4 strata, 2 unsampled
  automode-blocked  0 of 36
  automode-unavailable  0 of 130
  permission-rule  1 of 242
  user-rejected  1 of 156
```

The guarantee is therefore sharper than "proportional": **every category appears whenever the limit
is at least the number of strata**, and below that the shortfall is *disclosed* (`2 unsampled`)
rather than hidden. Reading a prior record cost less than the sweep that would have produced the
wrong prediction's refutation.

## Measurement: why the accuracy half cannot be run on this corpus

The experiment needs one property that satisfies **all three** conditions at once:

1. a rare category that random **misses often enough** that the arms can differ;
2. that category holding **enough instances to split** — at least one to seed the prompt *and*
   enough held out to measure recall on;
3. **content the label is inferable from**, or both arms score at chance and neither the sample nor
   the mode is what is being measured.

`P(a random sample of n contains zero of the K rare items) = C(N-K, n) / C(N, n)`, evaluated as the
product `prod(i=0..n-1) (N-K-i)/(N-i)` to avoid factorial overflow (node v24.18.0):

| property | rare class | N | K | n=8 | n=12 | n=20 | n=30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `decision.reversibility` | `irreversible` | 64 | **1** | 0.8750 | 0.8125 | **0.6875** | 0.5313 |
| `decision.reversibility` | `costly` | 64 | 11 | 0.2002 | 0.0812 | **0.0103** | 0.0004 |
| `tool_denial.denial_kind` | `automode-blocked` | 564 | 36 | 0.5880 | 0.4495 | **0.2611** | 0.1309 |

Against the three conditions, **the corpus closes like a pair of scissors**:

| candidate | (1) random misses it | (2) splittable | (3) classifiable content | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `decision` / `irreversible` | **yes, 68.8 % at n=20** | **NO — K=1** | yes, rich prose | **fails (2)** |
| `decision` / `costly` | **NO — 1.03 % at n=20** | yes, K=11 | yes, rich prose | **fails (1)** |
| `tool_denial` / `automode-blocked` | **yes, 26.1 % at n=20** | yes, K=36 | **NO** | **fails (3)** |

Condition (3) is the one worth showing, because it is the least guessable. `tool_denial` declares
six properties (`asc types show tool_denial`): `denial_kind`, `occurred_at`, `project`, `session_id`,
`tool_name`, `tool_use_id` — three of which are identifiers and one a timestamp. The two with
semantic content are not enough:

```
$ asc explore tool_denial --filter "evidence_text IS NOT NULL AND evidence_text != ''"
filter  matched 0 of 564 entries
```

**Zero of 564 `tool_denial` entries carry any prose** — the type is machine-derived by
`asc ingest claude-code`, which records the event, not a narration of it. That leaves `tool_name`
and `project` as the entire feature space, and `tool_name` is nearly constant:

```
$ asc explore tool_denial --group-by tool_name
91.0% (95% CI 88.3-93.1%, n=564)  Bash   513
```

**Bash covers 513 of 564 (91.0 %)** and spans all four denial kinds, so a classifier given
`(tool_name, project)` is predicting the majority class within an almost-empty cell. Both arms would
score near the base rate, and any gap between them would measure prompt noise rather than sampling.

The contrast is a property of the corpus, not of one type: the prose lives in the small
hand-recorded types and the volume lives in the machine-derived ones.

```
$ asc explore decision --filter "evidence_text IS NOT NULL AND evidence_text != ''"
showing 3 of 38, 7.9%          <- 38 of 64 decisions carry prose
```

Entries carrying any `evidence_text`, whole corpus: **103**, across seven types (`decision` 38,
`stage_transition` 19, `user_correction` 19, `evidence_record` 14, `note` 8, `stuck_event` 3,
`review_completed` 2). Every type large enough to sample from is a type with nothing to read.

## Decision

**KEEP `--sample stratified`. The bead's drop rule does not fire, and this record does not close the
bead.** Three separate statements, none of which should be collapsed into the others:

1. **The drop condition is not met.** It reads *"if stratified shows no advantage."* Stratified
   shows a large, measured selection advantage — 0/200 draws omitting a value against random's
   200/200 (EV-12), and a floor guarantee re-verified against today's binary above. Dropping the
   mode on the strength of an unrun experiment would discard a measured advantage to avoid an
   unmeasured one.
2. **The accuracy question is unanswered, and is not answerable on this corpus.** Stated as a
   requirement rather than a complaint: it needs a categorical property with **K ≥ ~20 instances of
   a category holding ≤ 10 % of the population**, on a type whose entries carry text the label is
   inferable from. No property here has all three. This is the same `n = 1` limitation EV-12
   recorded — one corpus, one user, one machine — arriving in a form that blocks a specific
   experiment rather than merely qualifying a result.
3. **`asc-asq` stays open, re-scoped to that requirement**, and is blocked on a corpus rather than
   on work. It should not be closed as "done", and the mode it governs should not be dropped
   meanwhile.

**The losing arm's numbers, reported as the bead requires.** The arm that loses here is the
prediction, not a sampler: **P2 was refuted** — proportional allocation does *not* round a rare
stratum to zero, because a floor is paid before rounding, and the mode's guarantee holds down to
`limit = number of strata` rather than degrading at small n. The probe that would have "discovered"
this cost one command against a record that already contained it.

**Confidence**  **High** for the infeasibility determination and for the floor: both rest on counts
                and exact combinatorics over the whole corpus, and every number above was produced
                by a command quoted beside it, driven through the real binary against the live
                store. The `0 of 564` and `513 of 564` figures are total-population counts, not
                samples, so they carry no sampling error.

                **None** for the bead's actual question. Nothing here measures classification
                accuracy under either arm, and no claim is made about it in either direction.

**Limitations**
- The three conditions are a *sufficient* account of why this corpus cannot decide the question, not
  a proof that no weaker design could. A design that classifies from `(tool_name, project)` and
  accepts near-chance accuracy in both arms would technically run; it would measure prompt noise,
  and is rejected on that ground rather than on impossibility.
- `decision.costly` at K=11 is below this project's `MIN_N` of 20
  (`packages/analysis/src/proportion.ts:50`). Even had condition (1) held, per-category recall
  measured on ~7 held-out items would be an anecdote, and would have had to be labelled one.
- The floor re-verification is three draws at one seed against the current store, confirming EV-12's
  200-draw result still holds in today's code. It is a spot check of a prior measurement, not an
  independent replication of it.
