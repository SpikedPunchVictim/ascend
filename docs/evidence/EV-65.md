# EV-65: how early in a session is the size flag knowable?

**Status** Predictions registered 2026-10-13 in the notes of `asc-09wx.4`, before any transcript
edit was counted. Measured the same day.

**Question** EV-63 found that a commit above its repository's 90th-percentile size is reworked more
often in our own repositories. Commit time is late for a nudge. Replaying our transcripts, how long
before the commit does the work in progress cross that threshold, and can the event stream see it?

## What was already known

EV-61 graded 817 session commits, 54 of them over their repository's size threshold (6.6%). Nothing
about when their lines were written had been looked at.

## Method (fixed before measuring)

- **Commits:** the 817 graded in EV-61, re-derived by the same script with each commit's sha, source
  files and, per file, the time of the previous git commit that touched it.
- **Edits:** every `Edit` or `Write` result in any transcript whose path is one of a commit's source
  files and whose time falls after that file's previous commit and at or before this one. Lines
  counted are the `+` lines of the result's `structuredPatch`, or the content lines of a create.
- **Knowable:** the first edit at which the running sum exceeds the commit's threshold. Lead is the
  minutes, and the tool calls in the committing session, between that edit and the commit.
- **Coverage:** running sum at the commit divided by the commit's added source lines. Changes made
  by a shell command are invisible to this method and show up as a ratio under 1.

**Rule.** An in-session size nudge is worth building only if the running sum crosses the threshold
for at least 70% of size-flagged commits and the median lead is at least 10 tool calls.

## Predictions

- **P1.** Among size-flagged commits the median coverage ratio is between 0.8 and 1.3.
- **P2.** The running sum crosses the threshold before the commit for at least 70% of them.
- **P3.** Where it crosses, the median lead is at least 10 minutes and at least 10 tool calls.
- **P4.** Among commits not size-flagged, the running sum crosses for at most 10%.
- **P5.** The crossing is late: median position above 50% of the time from the first counted edit
  to the commit.

## Decision

**By the rule, no in-session nudge is built on edit events.** The stream of `Edit` and `Write`
results accounts for a third of large commits. The rest were written, at least in part, some other
way: 9 of 54 have no edit on their files anywhere in a week, 4 were edited in another checkout of
the same repository, 3 before the file's previous commit, and 21 are only partly visible.

Two things the refuted predictions say that are worth keeping:

1. **Where the stream does see the work, the flag is knowable early.** In all 17 cases the commit
   came at least 20 tool calls after the threshold was passed, and the crossing sat at a median 22%
   of the way through. Lateness is not the obstacle; visibility is.
2. **The running sum almost never cries wolf**: 1 of 763 commits under the threshold crossed it.

So the size signal should be read from the working tree and not from the event stream.
`git diff --numstat` against the last commit sees every line however it was written, in whichever
checkout the session is in. That changes `asc-09wx.9` and `asc-09wx.10`: the recorder and the
trigger need a hook that measures the tree on a cadence, because a handler over `file.changed`
would miss two large commits in three.

## Confidence

**High** that edit events under-count: 26 of 54 large commits show no edit on their files inside
the window, a whole-population count. **Low** for the lead times, which rest on 17 commits, 9 of
them from one repository.

**Limitations.** The 817 commits are the 43% of session commits EV-61 could join to a repository
commit. `+` lines over-count a line edited twice, which would make crossing look earlier and more
frequent than it is; the 1-in-763 false-alarm figure says this is small. The previous-commit bound
is taken over all branches, so a commit rebased or cherry-picked after the edits cuts its own
window (the "before the file's previous commit" row). The diagnostic was designed after seeing the
result.
