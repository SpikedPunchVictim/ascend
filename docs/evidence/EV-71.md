# EV-71: what does the segment-level clustering cost the commit-risk nudge, and what does widening the rework trace buy?

**Status**      Measured 2026-10-15 for `asc-09wx.12` and `asc-lspj`. Predictions P1-P5 were
                registered in `asc-lspj` before any measurement ran; not sealed by sha256. One
                correction was made to a figure produced during the run and is marked in place
                below rather than folded in silently. Nothing was added afterwards.

**Question**    `EV-63` established that repo-relative size predicts rework in our own
                repositories -- pooled risk ratio 1.601, 95% interval [1.439, 1.782], over 1,062
                eligible commits. `asc-09wx.12` proposes to act on that with a holdout nudge. What
                it could not say is whether an experiment on the nudge is *answerable*: the unit a
                hook randomizes is the compaction segment, and an entire session's context is shared
                by every segment in it, so the effective sample size may be far below the unit
                count. `asc-6ola.4` and `asc-0hys` named the segment as the unit and the session as
                the cluster, but nobody had measured the correction for *this* outcome.
                `dogfood/0115` then found that `asc handlers power` cannot plan a nudge at all --
                a `say:` handler compiles to a one-field emit, so `successes == units` and the
                command refuses before reaching any clustering arithmetic. The design effect
                therefore had to be read off the replay directly.

## Method

Read-only. `asc handlers power` refuses every `say:` handler (`asc-xg62`, `dogfood/0115`), so this
measured the two halves separately and composed them.

- **The unit structure**, from `packages/cli/src/handler-replay.ts` via
  `spike/rework-design-effect/units.mjs`. The probe is the post-commit moment that
  `handlers/session-commit.yaml` records as the closest predicate to the nudge: `on: command.run`,
  `where head eq git + argv contains commit`. The clustering level is `sessionOf(unit)` --
  the part of the unit key before the first NUL, i.e. the session.
- **The outcome**, `reworked_7` as `EV-62` defines it: a later commit rewrites one of the commit's
  source lines within 7 days. Not observable from transcripts, so it was joined to the units by
  `EV-61`'s route -- the `[branch sha]` line from the commit result, plus a +/-1 s time window --
  re-derived over the current corpus rather than reused, `EV-61`'s published snapshot being two
  days older than the transcripts.
- **The design effect**, `clusterDesign` in `packages/analysis/src/design-effect.ts`:
  `designEffect = 1 + (sizeFactor - 1) * rho`, `sizeFactor = sum(m_i^2) / n`.
- **The aggregation rule was NOT pre-registered, and this is a real gap in the design.** A unit
  holds several commit triggers (mean 5.4), and `clusterDesign` accepts only a 0/1 per unit. Four
  rules are reported: `first`, `last`, `any`, `all`, each over the unit's source-bearing,
  window-closed commits.
- **Two populations.** *Narrow*: the units that carry a gradeable `reworked_7` under `EV-63`'s
  eligibility filter (source files present, at most 30 files). *Widened*: the units that carry a
  gradeable `reworked_7` once the filter is lifted and the four grizzly clones `EV-63` excluded are
  traced. One population is deliberately **not** reported: the 489-unit ceiling is not computable
  today (95 units have 7-day windows that have not closed) and was censored, not imputed -- see
  Confidence.
- **The rework traces** are `EV-63`'s, which survive only in an OS temp tree (`asc-0qbw`). They
  were run in place, never copied into the repo. `python3 -I` throughout.

**The bar:** a design effect low enough that the arm is answerable inside a horizon worth running,
and -- the thing that actually decided it -- enough gradeable units per active day to accrue the
required sample.

## Predictions

Registered in `asc-lspj` before measuring.

P1 `rhoSource` is `'estimated'`, not `'inapplicable'` or `'assumed-perfect'`.
P2 `sizeFactor` is dominated by skew: the largest cluster holds at least 15% of units.
P3 `designEffect` is at least 5.
P4 `designEffect` is below 18.614, the sibling `edit-unverified` handler's value on the same corpus.
P5 fewer than half the units resolve to a gradeable outcome (fewer than 430 of 852).

## Predictions against the result

**P1 -- confirmed.** Every cell reports `rhoSource: 'estimated'`. On the widened population there
are 24 clusters for 394 units, so `k >= 2` and `N > k` hold and the estimator applies.

**P2 -- confirmed.** The largest cluster holds 142 of 852 units, 16.67%, against the 15% bar, and
`size_factor` is 72.836 -- against the ~26 an even split across 33 clusters would give. The skew is
the dominant term in the product.

**P3 -- refuted under the primary rule.** 3.336 is below 5. It is confirmed only under `any`
(5.282) and `last` (5.012), and refuted again under `all` (4.638). The verdict is rule-dependent,
which is itself the answer to P3 and the reason the rule is now the open decision.

**P4 -- confirmed.** Every rule on both populations sits well under the sibling `edit-unverified`
handler's 18.614 (3.34-6.14). The prediction that this outcome would cluster more cheaply than the
capture handler was right, and by a wide margin.

**P5 -- confirmed, and hard.** 104 of 852 units, 12.2%, carry a gradeable outcome under `EV-63`'s
rule. The prediction holds on "gradeable", not on "resolves": 614 of 852 (72%) do resolve to some
commit, and it is the source-line requirement and the join, not resolution, that bind.

## Decision

**The design effect was never the constraint, and the pre-registered arm was mis-sized for the
wrong reason.** The measurement that looked fatal -- 472 active days under the narrow rule -- was
an artefact of an unwidened trace. Widening the trace to every source-bearing commit in all eight
repositories, which costs about four minutes of wall clock, brings it to 74 days at a 30%
reduction. `asc-09wx.12` is re-specified around that widening: widen the trace, then decide the
aggregation rule, then re-register the arm's baseline and units/day. It is not closed.

Two things the widening taught that no cost estimate would have surfaced:

- **A falling rho is not a falling design effect.** `rho` fell 57% (0.1613 -> 0.0690) while
  `designEffect` *rose* 7.8% (3.336 -> 3.596). Twelve more sessions appeared -- the between-cluster
  degrees of freedom the narrow estimate lacked -- but the units landed in more *and larger*
  sessions, and `largestCluster` went 23 to 63, taking `sizeFactor` from 15.48 to 38.61. The size
  factor won the product. Reading the rho drop as the headline would have reported the design as
  57% cheaper when it became slightly dearer.
- **42.6% of units can never carry a grade, at any price.** 125 of them resolve only to commits
  that touch no source line, so there is nothing to rewrite; 238 resolve to no commit at all,
  which is a *join* problem rather than a tracing one. The ceiling is 489 units, 10.19 per active
  day. Any cost estimate that averages these two buckets together with merely-expensive commits
  will report a budget for something that is impossible.

**The largest remaining lever is the aggregation rule, worth 139 days** (70 under `any` to 209
under `all`), against 398 for the widening itself. It was not pre-registered, and it is a design
decision rather than a measurement one.

## Confidence

**High** in the unit structure, the funnel, the rework outcome and the day counts -- the `EV-63`
cross-check reproduces to the digit on all four repositories, and the widened harness reproduces
the narrow measurement exactly (n 104, rho 0.16132642, designEffect 3.3361307), which is what makes
them comparable at all. The `deff` arithmetic was re-derived independently from `rho` and
`sizeFactor` for both populations and checks (3.3361, 3.5962).

**Low in the day figures as schedule.** They scale linearly in the design effect, and the design
effect is measured on the population available today. The 489-unit ceiling could not be measured --
95 of its units have 7-day windows that have not closed -- and was censored rather than imputed,
because `clusterDesign` accepts only 0/1 and any imputation of those 95 would have injected
between-cluster variance and no within-cluster variance, the exact quantity rho estimates. Three
imputations were computed as labelled artifacts and all give a *worse* design (6.152 to 9.366) than
the 3.596 measured today; they do not bracket the truth and none should be carried forward. The
settled value is unknown until the windows close, mid- to late October.

**Known weakness: 80% of the join is the weak route.** Of the joined triggers, 369 were matched by
`[branch sha]` and 1,570 by the +/-1 s time window. A mis-attributed commit moves an outcome between
sessions and moves rho directly. The sha-only arm was computed and disagrees materially with the
all-join arm (rho 0.3409 against 0.1613 on the narrow population), but it rests on 32 units in 4
sessions and is an anecdote, not a finding. It is stated here because it is the largest unquantified
error in the whole measurement, not because it was resolved.

**Not measured at all:** the intervention's *effect*. This is the design effect and the accrual
rate, which bound whether an experiment could answer the question. `EV-63`'s pooled ratio 1.601 is
the premise a nudge would act on, and it is measured at commit level, not at the unit level this
experiment would randomize.
